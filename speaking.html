Speaking practice

Ideas, tips, mistakes to avoid, 50 practice prompts with selfie recording, and 60 tongue twisters.

Ideas, tips and exercises

What to do
  • Speak daily for 10 minutes. Short and regular beats long and rare.
  • Answer with a reason and an example. “I like trains. They are cheap, and last Eid I travelled home for only 300 taka.”
  • Use linking words: because, however, for example, as a result.
  • Shadowing: play a short clip, then repeat it at the same speed and rhythm.
  • Record, listen, fix one thing each time (a sound, a tense, or a filler word).
What not to do
  • Don't memorise long answers. Examiners may notice rehearsed answers.
  • Don't stay silent to find the perfect word. Say “What I mean is…” and explain it simply.
  • Don't speak too fast. Clear and steady is better.
  • Don't answer with one word. “Yes.” becomes “Yes, because I find it relaxing.”
Where we make mistakes (with examples)
  • Tense: ✗ “Yesterday I go to market.” ✓ “Yesterday I went to the market.”
  • Subject–verb agreement: ✗ “People is happy.” ✓ “People are happy.”
  • Articles: ✗ “She is engineer.” ✓ “She is an engineer.”
  • Gerund / infinitive: ✗ “I enjoy to read.” ✓ “I enjoy reading.”
  • Word stress: pho-TOG-ra-phy, not PHO-to-graphy.
  • Fillers: too many “um, like, you know”. Pause silently instead.
  • Sounds: /θ/ in “think” is not /t/; /v/ in “very” is not /b/ or /w/.

Detailed speaking guide

1. How the IELTS Speaking test works (11–14 minutes)

Part 1 (4–5 min): short questions about home, study, work and hobbies. Part 2 (3–4 min): a cue card; you get 1 minute to prepare and speak for 1–2 minutes. Part 3 (4–5 min): deeper, abstract questions linked to Part 2.

2. The 4 scoring criteria and what to do
  • Fluency and coherence: keep talking, even slowly. Use linkers such as “firstly”, “on top of that” and “as a result”.
  • Vocabulary: use topic words and a few precise ones.
  • Grammar: mix simple and complex sentences.
  • Pronunciation: clear word stress, rhythm and intonation matter more than copying a particular accent.
3. Part 1: the Answer–Reason–Example method

Question: Do you like cooking?
✗ “Yes.”
✓ “Yes, I do. It helps me relax after studying. For example, on Fridays I usually cook rice and chicken curry for my family.”
Aim for 2–3 sentences.

4. Part 2: the cue card structure

Cue card: Describe a place you visited. Use your 1 minute to write 5 keywords, not sentences. Then speak in this order: What and where → when and who with → what you did → why it was special.

5. Part 3: the PEEL method

Point → Explain → Example → Link back. Question: Why do people travel? “Mainly to relax. Daily life is stressful, so a change of scene helps people recharge. For instance, my uncle visits the hills after busy months at work. That is why travel is so popular.”

6. Pronunciation: stress, linking and intonation
  • Word stress: pho-TOG-ra-phy, de-TE-ri-o-rate, ENG-lish.
  • Linking: “turn off” flows together; “an apple” links naturally.
  • Intonation: use natural pitch changes instead of a flat voice.
  • Sounds: practise /θ/, /v/, /w/, /r/ and /l/ carefully.
7. Natural fillers you may use

“Well,” “Let me see,” “That is a good question,” “To be honest,” and “What I mean is…”. Avoid repeating them too often.

8. A 7-day practice plan (20 minutes a day)
  • Day 1: read two model scripts aloud and copy the rhythm.
  • Day 2: answer 10 Part 1 questions.
  • Day 3: speak for 2 minutes and record yourself.
  • Day 4: practise PEEL on 5 questions.
  • Day 5: practise 10 tongue twisters, slowly and then faster.
  • Day 6: repeat a Part 2 topic and compare your recordings.
  • Day 7: complete a full mock test.

Model scripts to learn from

Read each aloud, then use the same structure with your own ideas. Do not memorise word for word.

Your hometown

I come from Sylhet, a green city surrounded by tea gardens. It is quite small, so everyone knows each other, and that gives it a warm feeling. What I love most is the weather. Honestly, nothing beats a cloudy morning there with a cup of tea. However, it can be crowded during festivals. All in all, it is a place I will always call home.

A person you admire

The person I admire most is my mother. She works as a teacher, and she has taught me to stay patient. For example, when I failed an exam last year, she did not shout. Instead, she helped me plan again. That is why I look up to her.

Your favourite food

My favourite food is biryani. It is a rice dish with meat and spices, and it smells amazing. We usually make it on special occasions, so it reminds me of family time. To be honest, I could eat it every day, although it is not very healthy.

A memorable journey

Last winter I travelled to Cox's Bazar by bus. It took about ten hours, and I was exhausted. But when I saw the sea at sunrise, I forgot everything. What made it special was that I went with my closest friends. If I had the chance, I would go again.

Learning English

I started learning English at school, but I improved when I began speaking daily. At first I felt shy, so I recorded myself. Listening back helped me notice my mistakes. These days I watch videos and repeat sentences, which has made me more confident.
