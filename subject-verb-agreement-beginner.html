Subject–verb agreement

Practise compound subjects, indefinite pronouns, collective nouns, quantities and a quiz.

1. Find the real subject

The verb agrees with the main subject, not with a nearby noun in a phrase beginning with of, with, together with, as well as or in addition to.

SentenceSubjectCorrect verb
The quality of the products ___ improving.quality (singular)is
The results of the experiment ___ reliable.results (plural)are
The professor, along with two students, ___ attending.professor (singular)is

Ignore the words between the subject and the verb when identifying the main subject.

2. Subjects joined by conjunctions

StructureUsual agreementExample
A and BPluralResearch and innovation are essential.
Either A or BUsually agrees with the nearer subjectEither the manager or the assistants are coming.
Neither A nor BUsually agrees with the nearer subjectNeither the assistants nor the manager is coming.
A or B (alternatives)Often agrees with the nearer subjectNeither the teacher nor the students are available.

When the result sounds awkward, rewrite the sentence to make the subject and verb easier to follow.

3. Indefinite pronouns

Usually singularUsually pluralExamples
everyone, everybody, someone, somebody, each, either, neitherboth, few, many, severalEveryone is ready. Several reports contain errors.

Some expressions depend on the noun after of: all, some, most, none.

  • Some of the water is contaminated.
  • Some of the samples are contaminated.

4. Collective nouns

Words such as team, family, committee, staff and government name groups. Use singular agreement when the group acts as one unit. British English may use plural agreement when members are viewed as individuals; usage differs by variety and context.

One unitMembers as individuals (common in British English)
The committee has made its decision.The committee are arguing among themselves.

5. Amounts, time and distance

A sum, measurement, or period considered one total usually takes a singular verb.

One totalSeparate countable items
Ten kilometres is a long distance.Ten kilometres of roads were inspected.
Five years is a long time.The five years they spent abroad were challenging.

6. There is / there are

In these structures, the verb agrees with the noun phrase that follows.

There is one reason. / There are several reasons.

In formal writing, avoid using “there's” with a plural noun if you want a consistently formal style.
