Gerunds and infinitives

Learn when to use verb + -ing, to + verb, and the bare infinitive, with clear rules, examples, Bangla explanations, and IELTS practice.

বাংলায় সহজ ব্যাখ্যা: ইংরেজিতে একটি verb-এর পরে আরেকটি verb এলে সব সময় একই form ব্যবহার করা যায় না। কোথাও reading, কোথাও to read, আবার কোথাও শুধু read ব্যবহার করতে হয়। প্রথম verb, preposition এবং বাক্যের অর্থ দেখে সঠিক form বেছে নিতে হয়।

1. Understand the basics

There are three important forms to learn first.

Form Structure Example বাংলা অর্থ
Gerund Verb + -ing I enjoy reading. আমি পড়তে আনন্দ পাই।
To-infinitive to + base verb I want to read. আমি পড়তে চাই।
Bare infinitive Base verb alone I can read. আমি পড়তে পারি।

What is a gerund?

A gerund is the -ing form of a verb used in a noun-like role.

Reading improves vocabulary.

Reading is the subject of the sentence.

I enjoy reading.

Reading is the activity that I enjoy.

Her favourite activity is swimming.

Swimming names an activity.

বাংলায়: Gerund দেখতে verb-এর -ing form-এর মতো, কিন্তু এটি noun-এর মতো কাজ করে। যেমন, Reading is useful বাক্যে reading একটি কাজ বা কার্যকলাপকে বোঝাচ্ছে।

What is an infinitive?

An infinitive is the base form of a verb. It can appear with to or without it.

I hope to study abroad.

To study is a to-infinitive.

You must study regularly.

Study is a bare infinitive after the modal must.

She made me laugh.

Laugh is a bare infinitive after made + object.

2. How to choose the correct form

Rule 1 Some verbs are followed by -ing.

I avoid wasting time.

Rule 2 Some verbs are followed by to + verb.

I decided to study.

Rule 3 A verb after a preposition takes -ing.

She is interested in learning.

Rule 4 Modal verbs usually take the bare infinitive.

He can swim. You should practise.

Rule 5 To + verb often expresses purpose.

I went to the library to study.

Important: these are patterns, not a single universal rule for every verb. Learn the verb together with the structure it requires.

These common verbs are normally followed by a gerund when another verb comes next.

মনে রাখুন: Enjoy, avoid, finish, suggest, consider এবং mind-এর পরে সাধারণত verb-এর -ing form বসে। তাই I enjoy reading সঠিক, কিন্তু I enjoy to read সাধারণত ভুল।

More examples

  • Researchers avoid making unsupported claims.
  • The government considered introducing a new policy.
  • She recommended using reliable sources.
  • He denied having copied the work.

5. Verbs followed by an object + to-infinitive

Some verbs need a person or object before the infinitive. This pattern is especially useful for IELTS Writing and Speaking.

Pattern Example
Want + person + toI want you to succeed.
Ask + person + toShe asked me to wait.
Tell + person + toHe told us to leave.
Encourage + person + toThey encouraged me to apply.
Allow + person + toThe rules allow students to use dictionaries.
Advise + person + toShe advised him to rest.
Invite + person + toThey invited us to attend.
Remind + person + toPlease remind me to call.
Persuade + person + toHe persuaded her to stay.

I want that you help me.

I want you to help me.

Use want + person + to-infinitive for this meaning.

Special case: suggest

  • Correct: She suggested going early.
  • Correct: She suggested that we go early.
  • Incorrect in standard usage: She suggested us to go early.

6. The bare infinitive

The bare infinitive is the base form of a verb without to.

After modal verbs

I can speak English.

You should practise every day.

We must finish this work.

Can, could, may, might, must, shall, should, will and would are followed by the base verb.

After make and let

The teacher made me rewrite the essay.

My parents let me travel alone.

Make and let + object + bare infinitive.

In the passive form of make, use to.

They made me wait.

I was made to wait.

After help

Both forms are correct in many contexts.

She helped me finish the task.

She helped me to finish the task.

After verbs of perception

With verbs such as see, hear and watch, the bare infinitive can describe the whole action. The -ing form can emphasize an action in progress.

I saw him cross the road.

I saw the action as a whole.

I saw him crossing the road.

I saw him while the action was in progress.

7. Prepositions followed by -ing

When a preposition is followed by a verb, use the -ing form.

Expression Example
Interested inI am interested in learning statistics.
Good atShe is good at explaining ideas.
Thinking ofHe is thinking of changing jobs.
Instead ofTry walking instead of driving.
BeforeWash your hands before eating.
AfterAfter finishing work, I went home.
ByYou can improve by practising.
WithoutHe left without saying goodbye.
DespiteDespite feeling tired, she continued.

Why is "look forward to meeting" correct?

In this expression, to is a preposition, not an infinitive marker.

I look forward to the holiday.

I look forward to meeting you.

A noun can follow the preposition to. A verb must take -ing.

Other examples include:

  • She is used to waking up early.
  • He objected to paying extra fees.
  • They are committed to improving education.
  • In addition to studying, she works part-time.
Exam tip: Do not assume that every occurrence of to introduces an infinitive. First identify the complete expression.

8. To-infinitive for purpose

Use to + base verb to explain why someone does something.

I went to the library to study.

Why did I go? To study.

She saves money to buy a laptop.

Why does she save money? To buy a laptop.

Governments should invest in transport to reduce congestion.

You can also use in order to or so as to for purpose.

  • Researchers collected additional data in order to improve accuracy.
  • He left early so as to avoid traffic.

9. Adjective patterns

Some adjectives are followed by a to-infinitive. Others are followed by a preposition and -ing.

Pattern Example
Happy + toI am happy to help.
Important + toIt is important to rest.
Easy + toThis book is easy to understand.
Ready + toShe is ready to begin.
Interested + inHe is interested in researching.
Good + atShe is good at writing.
Afraid + ofHe is afraid of failing.
Used + toI am used to working late.

Learn the full expression, not just the adjective. For example, happy to help and interested in helping follow different patterns.

10. Verbs that accept both forms

Some verbs can be followed by either -ing or to + verb. Sometimes the meaning changes significantly.

Stop

I stopped smoking.

I gave up the habit.

I stopped to smoke.

I paused another activity in order to smoke.

Remember

I remember meeting her.

I have a memory of meeting her.

Remember to lock the door.

Do not forget to perform the task.

Forget

I forgot to call him.

I failed to make the call.

I will never forget meeting him.

I will always remember that experience.

Try

Try restarting your phone.

Test this as a possible solution.

I tried to restart my phone.

I attempted to do it.

Regret

I regret saying that.

I am sorry about something I said.

We regret to inform you that your application was unsuccessful.

A formal expression used when giving unwelcome news.

Mean

This job means working late.

Working late is part of the job.

I meant to call you.

I intended to call you.

Go on

She went on talking.

She continued talking.

She went on to discuss the results.

She moved to the next topic.

Need

I need to clean the room.

I must perform the cleaning.

The room needs cleaning.

The room needs to be cleaned.

Like, love, hate, start and begin

These verbs can often take either form with little difference in meaning.

I like swimming.

I like to swim in the morning.

The -ing form often emphasizes general enjoyment. The infinitive can emphasize a habit, choice or preference. The difference is not absolute.

11. Advanced gerund forms

Perfect gerund

Structure: having + past participle.

He admitted having made a mistake.

The mistake happened before the admission.

She denied having taken the money.

The perfect gerund can emphasize that an action happened before the action of the main verb. In many contexts, the simple gerund is also acceptable.

Passive gerund

Structure: being + past participle.

He dislikes being criticized.

She is afraid of being misunderstood.

The report needs being checked.

The last example is not natural in many varieties of English. Prefer: The report needs checking, or The report needs to be checked.

Perfect passive gerund

Structure: having been + past participle.

He complained about having been treated unfairly.

The unfair treatment happened before the complaint.

12. Advanced infinitive forms

Form Structure Example
Simple infinitive to + base verb She hopes to finish.
Continuous infinitive to be + -ing He seems to be sleeping.
Perfect infinitive to have + past participle She seems to have forgotten.
Passive infinitive to be + past participle The work needs to be completed.
Perfect passive infinitive to have been + past participle He claims to have been invited.

These forms are more common in formal English, academic writing, and complex descriptions.

13. Gerunds and infinitives in IELTS

IELTS Speaking

  • I enjoy reading books in my free time.
  • I hope to pursue higher education abroad.
  • I am interested in learning about different cultures.
  • I would like to improve my communication skills.

IELTS Writing task 2

  • Investing in public transport can reduce traffic congestion.
  • Governments should aim to improve access to healthcare.
  • By promoting physical activity, cities can improve public health.
  • Many people avoid using public transport because of overcrowding.
  • It is essential to address the underlying causes of poverty.
IELTS tip: Accurate verb patterns are more valuable than using complicated structures incorrectly. Prioritize natural, grammatically correct sentences before trying to make every sentence advanced.

14. Common mistakes and corrections

I enjoy to read.

I enjoy reading.

Enjoy + gerund.

She suggested to leave.

She suggested leaving.

Suggest + gerund, or suggest that + clause.

I want going abroad.

I want to go abroad.

Want + to-infinitive.

He is looking forward to meet you.

He is looking forward to meeting you.

To is a preposition in this expression.

I am used to get up early.

I am used to getting up early.

Be used to + noun or gerund.

I decided going abroad.

I decided to go abroad.

Decide + to-infinitive.

She made me to laugh.

She made me laugh.

Make + object + bare infinitive in active sentences.

I want that you help me.

I want you to help me.

Want + person + to-infinitive.

He suggested me to apply.

He suggested that I apply.

Suggest does not normally take the pattern suggest + person + to-infinitive.

She can to swim.

She can swim.

Modal verbs take the bare infinitive.

15. Used to, be used to and get used to

These three expressions look similar but have different meanings.

Expression Meaning Example
Used to + base verb A past habit or state I used to play cricket.
Be used to + -ing Be accustomed to something I am used to working late.
Get used to + -ing Become accustomed to something I am getting used to waking early.
সহজে মনে রাখুন:
  • I used to wake up late. আগে দেরিতে উঠতাম।
  • I am used to waking up early. সকালে ওঠায় আমি অভ্যস্ত।
  • I am getting used to waking up early. সকালে ওঠার অভ্যাস তৈরি হচ্ছে।

16. Quick revision checklist

Do

  • Learn verbs in patterns.
  • Use -ing after prepositions.
  • Use to-infinitives after verbs such as want and decide.
  • Use the bare infinitive after modals.
  • Check whether the meaning changes.

Don't

  • Write enjoy to read.
  • Write suggest him to go.
  • Write can to swim.
  • Assume every to introduces an infinitive.
  • Memorize rules without examples.

18. Fill in the gaps

  1. I enjoy ___ (read) academic articles.
    Show answer

    reading

    Enjoy + -ing.

  2. She decided ___ (apply) for the scholarship.
    Show answer

    to apply

    Decide + to-infinitive.

  3. He is good at ___ (explain) difficult concepts.
    Show answer

    explaining

    At is a preposition.

  4. We went to the library ___ (study).
    Show answer

    to study

    To-infinitive expresses purpose.

  5. I look forward to ___ (meet) you.
    Show answer

    meeting

    To is a preposition.

  6. The teacher made us ___ (rewrite) the essay.
    Show answer

    rewrite

    Make + object + bare infinitive.

  7. She remembers ___ (visit) the museum as a child.
    Show answer

    visiting

    Remember + -ing describes a memory of a past experience.

  8. Remember ___ (submit) the application tomorrow.
    Show answer

    to submit

    Remember + to-infinitive refers to a task that must not be forgotten.

  9. The report needs ___ (check).
    Show answer

    checking or to be checked

    Both express that the report requires checking.

  10. He denied ___ (take) the documents.
    Show answer

    taking, or having taken if the earlier action needs emphasis.

20. Make the lesson stick

  • Learn five to ten verb patterns every week.
  • Write your own sentences with enjoy, avoid, decide, hope and suggest.
  • Practise pairs such as stop smoking and stop to smoke.
  • Read your sentences aloud to build natural speaking habits.
  • Review incorrect quiz answers after one day, three days and one week.
  • Use gerunds and infinitives in short IELTS Speaking answers and Task 2 paragraphs.
