1. The basics

A gerund and an infinitive are two common forms of English verbs. They can both function as subjects, objects, or complements, but the grammatical pattern and meaning determine which form you need.

Form Structure Example
Gerund verb + -ing Swimming is good exercise.
To-infinitive to + base verb I want to swim.
Bare infinitive base verb without to You should swim regularly.

Gerund versus present participle

Both forms end in -ing, but their grammatical functions differ.

Gerund: Reading improves vocabulary.

The -ing form acts as a noun and is the subject of the sentence.

Present participle: She is reading a novel.

The -ing form helps form the present continuous tense.

Present participle as an adjective: It was an interesting lecture.

How to form a gerund

Rule Base verb -ing form
Usually add -ing work working
Drop final silent -e make making
Double the final consonant in suitable cases run running
Change -ie to -y lie lying
Important: Spelling changes depend on the word and its final letters. For example, study → studying, but die → dying.

2. Gerunds: verb + -ing

Rule 1: A gerund can be the subject of a sentence

Gerund + verb + rest of sentence

Correct: Learning a language requires patience.

Correct: Exercising regularly improves physical health.

Correct: Collecting reliable data is essential for research.

Rule 2: A gerund can be the object of a verb

Some verbs are normally followed by a gerund rather than a to-infinitive.

Verb Example
admitHe admitted making a mistake.
avoidTry to avoid making unsupported claims.
considerThe team considered changing the method.
denyShe denied copying the report.
enjoyI enjoy reading academic articles.
finishThey finished collecting the data.
imagineImagine living without internet access.
keepHe kept asking questions.
mindWould you mind opening the window?
missI miss working with that team.
postponeThey postponed publishing the results.
practiseShe practises writing essays every day.
recommendThe researcher recommended collecting more data.
riskWe risk losing valuable information.
suggestHe suggested using a larger sample.
Common error: Do not automatically use a to-infinitive after every verb that describes an action.

Incorrect: I enjoy to read.

Correct: I enjoy reading.

Why? The verb enjoy is normally followed by a gerund.

Rule 3: A gerund follows a preposition

When a verb comes immediately after a preposition, it normally takes the -ing form.

  • She is interested in learning statistics.
  • He left without saying goodbye.
  • They succeeded in reducing the error rate.
  • I am looking forward to meeting the team.
  • She apologised for arriving late.

Rule 4: A gerund follows a possessive or object in some expressions

A possessive adjective or noun can sometimes indicate who performs the action.

I appreciate your helping me.

We were surprised by John's leaving early.

In everyday English, I appreciate you helping me is also common.

Rule 5: Gerunds follow common expressions

Expression Example
It is worthIt is worth reading the full report.
There is no point inThere is no point in arguing about it.
Cannot helpI cannot help laughing at that joke.
Feel likeI feel like taking a short break.
Have difficultyShe has difficulty understanding the graph.
Spend timeHe spends two hours studying every evening.
Be busyThey are busy preparing the presentation.

3. To-infinitives: to + base verb

Rule 1: Use a to-infinitive to express purpose

Subject + verb + to + base verb

I study English to improve my communication skills.

The government introduced the policy to reduce pollution.

Researchers collected additional data to validate the model.

You can often use in order to or so as to for a more explicit expression of purpose.

They expanded the sample in order to improve reliability.

She left early so as to avoid the traffic.

Rule 2: Use a to-infinitive after certain verbs

Verb Example
agreeThey agreed to cooperate.
aimThe study aims to identify key risk factors.
chooseShe chose to study statistics.
decideWe decided to conduct another analysis.
expectExperts expect temperatures to rise.
failHe failed to submit the form.
hopeI hope to pursue further research.
learnShe learned to interpret the results.
manageThey managed to complete the project.
offerHe offered to help.
planWe plan to publish the findings.
prepareThey prepared to present their results.
promiseShe promised to send the document.
refuseHe refused to answer.
seemThe findings seem to support the hypothesis.
wantI want to improve my writing.
would likeI would like to discuss the proposal.

Rule 3: Use a to-infinitive after adjectives

  • It is important to check the source.
  • She was delighted to receive the offer.
  • The problem is difficult to solve.
  • I am pleased to meet you.
  • It is essential to protect patient confidentiality.

Rule 4: Use a to-infinitive after question words

Common patterns include what to do, how to begin, and where to go.

I do not know what to write.

She explained how to use the software.

They could not decide where to meet.

He showed us which file to open.

Rule 5: Use a to-infinitive after nouns and pronouns in suitable patterns

I need something to drink.

She was the first person to arrive.

We have several issues to discuss.

The team needs a method to estimate the missing values.

Rule 6: Use too and enough with a to-infinitive

Pattern Example
too + adjective + to-infinitiveThe box is too heavy to lift.
adjective + enough + to-infinitiveShe is confident enough to present.
enough + noun + to-infinitiveWe have enough evidence to proceed.

4. Bare infinitives: the base verb without to

Rule 1: Use a bare infinitive after modal verbs

Modal verbs include can, could, may, might, must, shall, should, will, and would.

You should revise your essay.

Researchers must report their methods clearly.

She can analyse the data independently.

The results may indicate a seasonal pattern.

Incorrect: You should to revise your essay.

Correct: You should revise your essay.

Rule 2: Use a bare infinitive after make and let

The evidence made us reconsider the conclusion.

My supervisor let me choose the topic.

In the passive voice, make is followed by a to-infinitive.

Active: The teacher made him rewrite the essay.

Passive: He was made to rewrite the essay.

Rule 3: Use a bare infinitive after certain perception verbs

Verbs such as see, hear, watch, and feel can be followed by an object and a bare infinitive when the whole action is perceived.

I saw him cross the road.

We heard the audience applaud.

The -ing form can emphasise an action in progress rather than the complete event.

I saw him crossing the road.

This focuses on the action as it was happening; it does not necessarily imply that the speaker witnessed the entire crossing.

Rule 4: Use a bare infinitive after had better and would rather

You had better leave now.

I would rather stay at home.

We would rather analyse the original data.

Remember: Ought to is different. It takes a to-infinitive: You ought to check the results.

5. Verbs followed by a gerund or infinitive

Some verbs allow both forms with little change in meaning

Verb Gerund To-infinitive
begin It began raining. It began to rain.
continue She continued working. She continued to work.
hate I hate waiting. I hate to interrupt.
like I like reading. I like to read before bed.
love He loves travelling. He loves to travel.
prefer I prefer walking. I prefer to walk.
start It started snowing. It started to snow.

With like, love, hate, and prefer, the gerund often describes enjoyment or general preference. The to-infinitive can describe a habit, choice, or action viewed in a particular situation. The difference is not absolute.

Some verbs can take an object before the to-infinitive

Verb + object + to-infinitive
Verb pattern Example
advise someone to doThe doctor advised him to rest.
allow someone to doThe policy allows students to work part-time.
ask someone to doShe asked me to review the draft.
encourage someone to doThe teacher encouraged us to practise.
expect someone to doWe expect the model to perform well.
invite someone to doThey invited her to present the findings.
persuade someone to doHe persuaded them to reconsider.
remind someone to doPlease remind me to send the email.
teach someone to doShe taught me to use the software.
want someone to doI want you to check the calculation.

Some verbs take an object followed by a bare infinitive

The teacher made the students rewrite the paragraph.

Let the participants finish their answers.

We watched the children play outside.

Some verbs have different patterns depending on their meaning

For example, remember, forget, stop, try, regret, and go on can change meaning depending on the form that follows.

Study these contrasts in the next section.

6. Gerunds and infinitives that change meaning

Remember doing versus remember to do

Pattern Meaning Example
remember + gerund Recall a past action I remember meeting her at the conference.
remember + to-infinitive Remember that a task must be done Remember to submit the application.

Forget doing versus forget to do

Forget + gerund: I will never forget visiting the museum.

This means that the speaker will always remember the experience.

Forget + to-infinitive: I forgot to lock the door.

This means that the speaker failed to perform the task.

Stop doing versus stop to do

Stop + gerund: He stopped smoking.

He quit the habit.

Stop + to-infinitive: He stopped to smoke.

He stopped another activity in order to smoke.

Try doing versus try to do

Try + gerund: Try restarting the computer.

Experiment with this as a possible solution.

Try + to-infinitive: I tried to lift the box.

Make an effort to do something, whether or not it succeeds.

Regret doing versus regret to do

Regret + gerund: She regrets leaving the job.

She feels sorry about a past action.

Regret + to-infinitive: We regret to inform you that your application was unsuccessful.

This is a formal expression used when giving unwelcome information.

Go on doing versus go on to do

Go on + gerund: He went on talking for another hour.

He continued the same activity.

Go on + to-infinitive: After discussing the problem, she went on to explain the solution.

She moved to a different activity or stage.

Mean doing versus mean to do

Mean + gerund: Taking this job means moving to another city.

Moving is a consequence or requirement of taking the job.

Mean + to-infinitive: I meant to call you yesterday.

I intended to call, but perhaps I did not.

Need doing versus need to do

Need + gerund: The report needs editing.

The report needs to be edited.

Need + to-infinitive: I need to edit the report.

I must perform the action.

Compare the patterns

Verb Verb + -ing Verb + to-infinitive
remember Recall a past event Remember a task
forget Fail to retain a memory Fail to perform a task
stop End an activity Pause another activity to do something
try Experiment with a method Make an effort
regret Feel sorry about an action Formally introduce unwelcome news or a difficult statement
go on Continue the same activity Move to another activity or stage
mean Involve or entail Intend

7. Prepositions, fixed expressions, and infinitives

The important difference: preposition to versus infinitive to

The word to does not always introduce an infinitive. Sometimes it is a preposition. When it is a preposition, a following verb normally takes the -ing form.

Expression Correct pattern Example
look forward to to + gerund I look forward to hearing from you.
be used to to + gerund or noun She is used to working under pressure.
get around to to + gerund I finally got around to writing the report.
object to to + gerund They objected to changing the schedule.
devote time to to + gerund or noun She devotes time to learning English.
in addition to to + gerund or noun In addition to teaching, he conducts research.

Incorrect: I look forward to meet you.

Correct: I look forward to meeting you.

Why? In this expression, to is a preposition.

Be used to versus used to

Structure Meaning Example
used to + base verb A past habit or state that is no longer true I used to walk to school.
be used to + gerund/noun Be accustomed to something I am used to walking long distances.
get used to + gerund/noun Become accustomed to something I am getting used to working remotely.

Adjective + preposition + gerund

  • She is interested in conducting research.
  • He is good at solving statistical problems.
  • They are responsible for managing the project.
  • I am worried about missing the deadline.
  • We are committed to improving public health.
  • She is capable of handling complex datasets.

8. Advanced gerund and infinitive forms

Perfect gerund

having + past participle

A perfect gerund can emphasise that an action happened before the action expressed by the main verb.

He denied having copied the material.

She regretted having ignored the advice.

They admitted having made an error.

Passive gerund

being + past participle

He dislikes being interrupted.

The report needs being checked is not the usual standard pattern in most varieties of English. Prefer checking or to be checked, depending on the intended meaning.

She appreciates being included in the discussion.

Perfect passive gerund

having been + past participle

He resented having been excluded from the meeting.

She denied having been informed about the change.

Perfect infinitive

to have + past participle

The perfect infinitive can refer to an action that happened before the time expressed by the main verb.

She seems to have misunderstood the instructions.

He claims to have completed the analysis.

The team appears to have identified a new risk factor.

Passive infinitive

to be + past participle

The results need to be verified.

She hopes to be selected for the programme.

The paper is expected to be published next year.

Perfect passive infinitive

to have been + past participle

The data appear to have been collected incorrectly.

The findings are believed to have been influenced by seasonal variation.

Negative gerunds and infinitives

Place not before the gerund or infinitive phrase.

Not + gerund: He admitted not knowing the answer.

Not + to-infinitive: She decided not to participate.

Not + perfect gerund: He denied not having received the email.

Split infinitives

A split infinitive occurs when a word, often an adverb, comes between to and the base verb.

The team hopes to quickly identify the source of the problem.

This is acceptable in modern English when it sounds natural and keeps the intended meaning clear.

9. Using gerunds and infinitives in IELTS

IELTS Writing Task 1

The chart illustrates the proportion of people using public transport.

The percentage continued to increase over the period.

The figure began to decline after 2015.

Collecting accurate data is essential for interpreting the trend.

IELTS Writing Task 2

Gerund as subject: Investing in public transport can reduce congestion.

Purpose infinitive: Governments should improve cycling facilities to encourage sustainable travel.

Verb pattern: Many researchers recommend introducing evidence-based policies.

Preposition + gerund: By improving access to education, societies can reduce inequality.

Advanced pattern: Authorities need to consider the long-term consequences of expanding urban areas.

IELTS Speaking

I enjoy reading because it helps me expand my vocabulary.

I hope to pursue a career in research.

I used to spend more time watching television, but now I prefer reading.

I am interested in learning about different cultures.

Academic and research writing

The study aims to investigate the association between climate and disease incidence.

Using spatial data can help researchers identify high-risk areas.

The authors recommend conducting further longitudinal studies.

The analysis was designed to evaluate the predictive performance of several models.

After adjusting for potential confounders, the association remained statistically significant.

IELTS tip: Use gerunds and infinitives accurately rather than forcing complicated structures into every sentence. Clear grammar, precise vocabulary, logical development, and appropriate sentence variety are more valuable than unnecessary complexity.

10. Common mistakes and corrections

Incorrect Correct Explanation
I enjoy to read. I enjoy reading. Enjoy is followed by a gerund.
She suggested to go. She suggested going. Suggest is normally followed by a gerund or a that-clause.
He wants going home. He wants to go home. Want is followed by a to-infinitive.
I look forward to meet you. I look forward to meeting you. Here, to is a preposition.
You should to study. You should study. A modal verb takes a bare infinitive.
She made me to apologise. She made me apologise. Active make + object takes a bare infinitive.
He avoided to answer. He avoided answering. Avoid is followed by a gerund.
They decided going home. They decided to go home. Decide is normally followed by a to-infinitive.
I am used to wake up early. I am used to waking up early. Be used to takes a noun or gerund.
I stopped to smoke last year. I stopped smoking last year. Stopping smoking means quitting the habit.
He suggested me to apply. He suggested that I apply. Suggest does not normally use this object + infinitive pattern.
She is interested to learn English. She is interested in learning English. Interested in is followed by a noun or gerund.

Three questions to ask yourself

  1. Does the preceding verb require a gerund or an infinitive?
  2. Is the word to a preposition or part of an infinitive?
  3. Does changing the form change the meaning?

11. Practice exercises

Complete each sentence with the correct form of the verb in brackets.

  1. She enjoys ________ (read) scientific articles.
  2. We decided ________ (conduct) another survey.
  3. He apologised for ________ (arrive) late.
  4. They made us ________ (wait) outside.
  5. I look forward to ________ (hear) from you.
  6. Remember ________ (lock) the door before leaving.
  7. He stopped ________ (smoke) because of health concerns.
  8. She tried ________ (restart) the computer to fix the issue.
  9. The report needs ________ (revise).
  10. It is important ________ (check) the results carefully.
Show answers and explanations
  1. reading: Enjoy is followed by a gerund.
  2. to conduct: Decide is followed by a to-infinitive.
  3. arriving: A verb after the preposition for takes the -ing form.
  4. wait: Active make + object takes a bare infinitive.
  5. hearing: To is a preposition in look forward to.
  6. to lock: Remember to do something means remember a task that must be performed.
  7. smoking: Stop smoking means quit the habit.
  8. restarting: Try doing something means test a possible solution.
  9. revising / to be revised: The report needs revising or needs to be revised.
  10. to check: The adjective pattern important + to-infinitive is correct.
