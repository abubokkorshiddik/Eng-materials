Subject–verb agreement

Build confidence with singular and plural verbs, simple rules and guided practice.

1. The basic rule

A verb must match its subject. A singular subject usually takes a singular verb; a plural subject takes a plural verb.

SubjectPresent simpleBe / have / do
Iworkam, have, do
He / She / Itworksis, has, does
You / We / Theyworkare, have, do
The student works every day.
The students work every day.
My brother has a laptop. My brothers have laptops.

2. How to find the subject

Ask “Who or what does the action?” Do not choose the verb by looking only at the nearest noun.

The cost of the books is high.
The pages of the book are torn.

The subject is cost in the first sentence and pages in the second.

3. Common patterns

Words like everyone and each

Everyone, everybody, someone, nobody, each and every take a singular verb.

Everyone is ready. Each student has a book.

People, children and police

People, children and police take plural verbs.

People are concerned. The children play outside. The police are here.

Uncountable nouns

Words such as advice, information, research, furniture and news normally take singular verbs.

The information is useful. The news was surprising.

Two subjects with and

Two separate subjects joined by and usually take a plural verb.

Mina and Rafi are classmates. Reading and writing are useful skills.

Do and does

Use does with he, she and it. The main verb after does stays in its base form.

Does she likes tea?
Does she like tea?
He don't agree.
He doesn't agree.

4. Beginner practice

1. The student ___ hard.

2. People ___ waiting outside.

3. The information ___ correct.

4. Everyone ___ a chance.

5. Does he ___ the answer?
