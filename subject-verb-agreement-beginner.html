Subject–verb agreement

Start with the basic rules, easy tables, examples and a ten-question quiz.

1. The basic idea

The subject tells us who or what a sentence is about. The verb tells us what the subject does or what state it is in. Subject–verb agreement means choosing a verb that matches the subject.

SubjectCorrect verbExample
IworkI work every day.
You / We / TheyworkThey work every day.
He / She / ItworksShe works every day.
One studentstudiesThe student studies English.
Two studentsstudyThe students study English.

Easy rule: In the present simple, add -s or -es to the verb with he, she, it, or a singular noun in affirmative sentences.

2. How to add -s or -es

VerbHe / She / ItExample
workworksHe works hard.
readreadsShe reads daily.
gogoesHe goes home.
watchwatchesShe watches films.
studystudiesHe studies statistics.

For most verbs, add -s. Add -es to verbs ending in sounds/spellings such as -ch, -sh, -s, -x, and often -o. For a consonant + y, change y to -ies.

3. The verb “be”

SubjectPresentPast
Iamwas
He / She / It / one personiswas
You / We / They / plural nounsarewere

I am ready. She is ready. They are ready. He was tired. They were tired.

4. Negatives and questions

With does or doesn't, the main verb returns to its base form.

CorrectIncorrectWhy?
She doesn't like tea.She doesn't likes tea.“Doesn't” already marks the third-person singular.
Does he work here?Does he works here?Use the base verb after does.
They don't live here.They doesn't live here.Use don't with they.

5. A quick checking method

  1. Find who or what performs the action.
  2. Decide whether the subject is singular or plural.
  3. Choose the matching verb form.
  4. Check whether do/does already carries the grammar.
