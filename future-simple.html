The most important rule

Use will + the base form of a verb to express many future predictions, promises, offers, and decisions made at the moment of speaking.

Example: I think she will pass the exam.

Subject + will + base verb

I will learn. · She will learn. · They will learn.

How to use this page: Start with the first question. Open each lesson card to see the answer. Use the small practice activity immediately after learning a rule. At the end, take the final quiz.

Important: English does not use will for every future sentence. Sometimes the present simple, present continuous, or going to is more appropriate. You will learn the differences below.

Part 1: understand the meaning

First, learn what will means and why speakers use it.

1What does will mean?
Simple answer

Will is a modal auxiliary verb. We often use it to talk about what we think, expect, decide, promise, or offer to do in the future.

Look at these examples

I will study tonight. The speaker is talking about a future action.
It will probably rain tomorrow. The speaker is making a prediction.
I will help you. The speaker is offering help or making a promise.

Does will always mean the same thing?

No. The meaning depends on the situation. In one sentence, will may express a prediction. In another, it may express willingness, a promise, or a decision.

2What is the base form of a verb?
Simple answer

The base form is the basic form of a verb, without endings such as -s, -ed, or -ing, and without to.

Base formOther formCorrect with will
gogoesI will go.
studystudiesShe will study.
writewroteThey will write.
beis / am / areHe will be ready.
havehasWe will have time.
dodoesShe will do it.

Why does this matter?

Unlike the present simple, the verb after will does not change for he, she, or it.

Correct: She will work. He will work. It will work. The verb work remains unchanged.
Remember: Do not write “She will works” or “He will to work.”

Part 2: build the sentence

Learn the three main forms: positive, negative, and question.

3How do I make a positive sentence?
The rule
Subject + will + base verb

She + will + study

Examples

I will learn English.Subject: I. Auxiliary: will. Verb: learn.
She will visit her grandmother.Use visit, not visits.
They will arrive tomorrow.The sentence describes a future event.
We will be ready soon.The base form of be is used after will.

Can I use contractions?

Yes. In everyday speaking and informal writing, will is often shortened.

Full formContraction
I willI'll
You willYou'll
He willHe'll
She willShe'll
We willWe'll
They willThey'll

Contractions are very common in listening and speaking. In formal IELTS Writing Task 1 and Task 2, full forms are generally a safer choice.

Try it yourself

Complete the sentence: My brother will _____ a doctor.

4How do I make a negative sentence?
The rule
Subject + will not + base verb

Short form: won't

Put not immediately after will. The verb that follows remains in its base form.

I will not forget your advice.The speaker says the action will not happen.
She won't attend the meeting.Won't means will not.
They will not agree with the decision.Agree remains in its base form.
He won't be late.Use be, not is or will be not.

Is won't formed in the usual way?

No. Will not contracts to won't, which is an irregular contraction. Learn it as a pair.

Common errors

Incorrect: She don't will come.

Correct: She will not come. / She won't come.

Incorrect: He won't goes.

Correct: He won't go.

Choose the correct negative sentence.

5How do I make a question with will?
The rule
Will + subject + base verb?

Will + you + help?

To make a yes or no question, move will to the beginning of the sentence, before the subject.

Will you help me?Asks whether the person is willing to help.
Will she join us tomorrow?Asks about a possible future event.
Will they be available next week?Be remains in its base form.

How do I answer a will question?

Use will or won't in a short answer.

Will you come?
Yes, I will. / No, I won't.

How do I ask a question with a question word?

Question word + will + subject + base verb?
When will you arrive?
Where will they stay?
What will she do after graduation?
How will the new policy affect students?
Do not use do or does with will in this structure.

Incorrect: Do you will come?

Correct: Will you come?

Part 3: when should I use will?

The form is easy once you understand it. The next step is choosing when it fits the meaning.

6When should I use will for predictions?
Simple answer

Use will when you predict or express an opinion about something that may happen in the future.

Examples from everyday life

I think it will rain tomorrow.A prediction about the weather.
People will use more renewable energy in the future.A prediction about society.
She will probably pass the examination.A prediction about an individual.
Technology will continue to change education.A prediction about a long-term trend.

Which words often appear with predictions?

These expressions often show that you are making a prediction, although they do not automatically require will.

I think · I believe · probably · perhaps · maybe · I expect · I am sure

Can I use will with probably?

Yes. Probably often appears after will and before the main verb.

She will probably arrive early.
They probably won't accept the offer.

In negative sentences, probably can come before won't or after won't. For example, “They probably won't come” and “They won't probably come” are not equally natural in all contexts. The first is generally the more natural choice.

Remember: A prediction is not a guarantee. Use words such as probably, possibly, and might when you want to show uncertainty.

Check your understanding

Which sentence expresses an opinion about the future?

7When should I use will for decisions, promises, and offers?
Simple answer

Use will when you decide to do something at the moment of speaking. You can also use it to offer help, make promises, or express willingness.

A. Decisions made at the moment of speaking

The phone is ringing. I'll answer it. The speaker decides now to answer the phone.
I'm thirsty. I'll get some water. The decision is made while speaking.

B. Offers

I'll carry that bag for you.The speaker offers assistance.
I'll help you understand this lesson.The speaker offers help.

C. Promises

I will never forget your kindness.The speaker makes a promise or strong commitment.
I promise I won't tell anyone.The speaker promises to keep something secret.

D. Willingness

Don't worry. I will explain it again.The speaker is willing to explain again.

What is the difference between a decision and a plan?

A decision made now often uses will. A plan or intention already formed before speaking often uses going to.

Decision now: The room is cold. I'll close the window.
Plan made earlier: I've decided that I'm going to study abroad next year.

Check your understanding

Your friend has several bags. You decide to help immediately. What would you say?

8Can will describe future facts and expectations?
Simple answer

Yes. Will can describe expected future events, future states, or things that are expected to happen.

The results will be published next Monday.An expected future event.
My university will open a new laboratory next year.A future event or development.
It will be difficult to solve this problem.An expectation about a future situation.

Is will compulsory for every future fact?

No. Timetables often use the present simple. Existing arrangements often use the present continuous. The best form depends on the type of event and the meaning.

The train leaves at 7 a.m.A timetable.
I'm meeting my supervisor tomorrow.An arranged meeting.
I think the meeting will be useful.A prediction or opinion.

Check your understanding

Which sentence is a natural way to report a timetable?

9Can will be used to make requests?
Simple answer

Yes. Will you ...? can be used to ask someone to do something. It is often used for requests, although could and would may sound more polite in some situations.

Will you open the window, please?A request for help.
Will you send me the document?A request to send something.
Will you please wait here?A request to wait.

Will you and Would you: what is the difference?

Both can be used for requests. Would you is often more polite or less direct.

Will you help me?A direct request.
Would you help me, please?Often a more polite request.

Part 4: which future form should I choose?

This is one of the most important parts of learning future forms. Several structures can refer to the future, but they do not always express the same meaning.

10What is the difference between will and going to?
Simple answer

Will is common for spontaneous decisions and predictions or opinions. Going to is common for plans already decided and predictions based on present evidence.

SituationExampleReason
Decision nowI'll call him now.The decision is made while speaking.
Earlier intentionI'm going to call him tonight.The intention already exists.
OpinionI think she will win.The speaker expresses a prediction.
Visible evidenceLook at those clouds. It's going to rain.The clouds provide evidence.

Are these rules absolute?

No. Both forms can sometimes express predictions. The difference is often the speaker's viewpoint and the evidence or intention being emphasised.

Try it yourself

You can see a glass falling off the table. Which prediction is especially natural?

11What is the difference between will and the present continuous?
Simple answer

The present continuous can describe future arrangements, especially when details have already been organised.

I'm meeting my teacher tomorrow. A meeting has been arranged.
I'll meet my teacher tomorrow. Depending on the context, this can express a decision, promise, or expectation.

How can I recognise an arrangement?

There may be a fixed time, an appointment, a booking, or an agreement with another person.

We're flying to Singapore next Friday.A travel arrangement.
She's seeing the dentist at 10 a.m.An appointment.

Do not assume every future sentence with tomorrow must use will. Think about the intended meaning first.

Try it yourself

You have already booked an appointment for tomorrow. Which sentence highlights the arrangement?

12What is the difference between will and the present simple?
Simple answer

The present simple is commonly used for timetables, schedules, and future time clauses. Will is often used for predictions and future expectations.

The bus arrives at 9 a.m.A timetable.
I think the bus will arrive soon.A prediction.
The course starts next Monday.A scheduled event.

What happens after when, before, after, until, and as soon as?

When referring to the future, English commonly uses the present simple in these time clauses, even when the main clause uses will.

When I arrive, I will call you.
I'll wait until she comes.
As soon as the results arrive, we will inform you.
Before you leave, I'll give you the documents.
Important distinction: A question beginning with when can still use will in the main question.

“When will you arrive?” is correct.

“When I arrive, I will call you” is also correct.

The first asks about the time of arrival. The second uses a time clause.

Try it yourself

Complete the sentence: When she _____ home, we will eat dinner.

Part 5: which time expressions can I use?

Time expressions help you understand when an event happens, but they do not decide the tense on their own.

ExpressionExampleMeaning
TomorrowI'll call you tomorrow.The next day.
Next weekIt will be ready next week.The following week.
SoonThings will improve soon.In the near future.
In the futurePeople will live differently in the future.An unspecified future period.
One dayOne day, I will visit Japan.An unspecified future day.
ProbablyShe will probably join us.A likely future event.
I thinkI think it will work.An opinion or prediction.
Remember: “Tomorrow” does not automatically mean will.

I'll call you tomorrow. This can express a decision or promise.

I'm meeting her tomorrow. This describes an arrangement.

The train leaves tomorrow. This can describe a timetable.

Part 6: what mistakes should I avoid?

Read each incorrect sentence, then compare it with the correct version and explanation.

IncorrectCorrectWhy?
She will goes home.She will go home.Use the base verb after will.
I will to study.I will study.Do not add to after will.
He will studies English.He will study English.Will does not take the third person -s ending.
She don't will come.She won't come.Form the negative with will not or won't.
Do you will help me?Will you help me?Put will before the subject in questions.
Will she can attend?Will she be able to attend?Do not place two modal verbs together in this structure.

How can I correct my own sentences?

  1. Find the subject, such as I, she, or they.
  2. Check whether the sentence needs will.
  3. Check that the verb after will is in its base form.
  4. For a negative sentence, check the position of not.
  5. For a question, check whether will comes before the subject.
  6. Check the meaning to see if another future form is more suitable.

Part 7: how can I use will in IELTS?

Grammar is more useful when you can apply it to real exam tasks. Learn how will works in speaking, writing, reading, and listening.

IELTS speaking

Use will to make predictions, discuss future ambitions, or respond spontaneously.

Question: How do you think education will change in the future? Answer: I think online learning will become more common, and students will have greater access to international courses.

Practice: How will your city change in the next ten years?

IELTS writing

Use will when discussing expected outcomes, future consequences, or predictions. Make sure your claims are supported and appropriately cautious.

Example: If governments invest in public transport, traffic congestion may decrease and air quality will likely improve.

Tip: You can use may, might, or could when a future outcome is uncertain.

IELTS reading

Notice whether the writer is describing an established fact, a plan, or a prediction. Words such as will, may, and might can change the strength of a claim.

Example: The researchers believe the new treatment will reduce recovery time. This is an expectation, not necessarily a confirmed result.

Tip: Do not treat a prediction as a fact simply because the sentence uses will.

IELTS listening

Listen for contractions and negative forms. They may be pronounced quickly in natural speech.

I'll = I will
They'll = They will
Won't = Will not

Tip: Pay close attention to won't because missing the negative can reverse the meaning.

Useful IELTS sentence patterns

Prediction: I believe that this trend will continue in the coming years.
Possible consequence: This development will likely have a positive effect on public health.
Uncertainty: These measures may reduce pollution, but their long-term effects remain uncertain.
Personal ambition: In the future, I hope I will be able to contribute to meaningful research.
