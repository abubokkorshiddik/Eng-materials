Past perfect continuous

Stress how long an activity had been going on before a past moment.

duration → past pointpastnowfuture

In plain words

Take the present perfect continuous and move it back in time. Use it when something had been going on for a while before another past event, and it helps explain that event.

How to build it

Positive had been + verb-ing

Negative had not been + verb-ing

Question Had + subject + been + verb-ing?

Key words for · since · all day · before · by the time

When we use it

Duration before a past point

I had been waiting for an hour when the bus came.

Duration up to that point.

Cause of a past state

The road was wet; it had been raining.

Earlier activity explains.

Effort before an event

They had been trying to call him.

Ongoing effort.

Tired, upset, etc.

He was exhausted: he had been working all night.

Explains earlier state.

Tenses that get mixed up with it

She had written the report.
She had been writing the report.

Result vs process.

They had lived there for years.
They had been living there for years.

Both fine; continuous foregrounds activity.

I was studying at 8.
I had been studying for two hours by 8.

Moment vs duration.

Time words are clues, not rules. The meaning you want decides the tense.

Mistakes people make

I had been work all day. → I had been working all day.

-ing after been.

They has been waiting. → They had been waiting.

had for all subjects.

I had been knowing him. → I had known him.

Stative.

studying since three hours → studying for three hours

for + duration, since + start.

Going deeper

Core idea: duration before a past point

Like the present perfect continuous, but shifted back: “At 8 I had been waiting for an hour.” You need a past reference point.

Cause and effect

“His eyes were red; he had been crying.” Earlier ongoing activity explains a past state.

Simple vs continuous

“She had written the report” (done). “She had been writing the report” (was working on it; not necessarily done). Counts and results use simple.

Reported speech

“I have been waiting for hours,” he said → He said he had been waiting for hours.

Shortcuts that work in exams

  • Past point + duration. = past perfect continuous.
  • Cause of past state. Use it to explain.
  • For/since. for 3 hours, since morning.
  • Stative verbs stay simple. had known, not had been knowing.

Where exams try to catch you

  • Too many past perfects: Use only when the order or duration matters.
  • “When” confusion: When I arrived, he had been waiting (earlier) vs was waiting (at that moment).
  • Since + finished time: Avoid: since yesterday works; since two hours is wrong.

What to do, and what to leave alone

Do

  • Use it for duration before a past point.
  • Use it for the cause of a past state.
  • Use for/since.
  • Check the past point exists.

Don't

  • Don't use it without a past reference.
  • Don't use stative verbs.
  • Don't use it for completed results.
  • Don't use have/has.

Why we get it wrong

Perfect vs continuous

He had been finishing.

Try this: had finished for results.

Missing been

had waiting.

Try this: had been waiting.

Time clause confusion

since two hours.

Try this: for two hours.

Small habits that help

  • Explain why you were tired yesterday with had been.
  • Mark reference point + duration in reading.
  • Reported speech: have been → had been.

In IELTS and real life

  • Speaking: “I was tired because I had been revising all night.”
  • Writing: use sparingly for duration before a past point.
  • Reading: find duration + past reference point.
  • Listening: had been + -ing = earlier ongoing process.

Real world: explain the background to a story or complaint.

TOEFL, PTE, GRE and bank or civil-service English tests ask the same thing: does the tense match the time you mean?

Practice

Spot the mistake

  1. She had been wait.
    Show answerShe had been waiting.: -ing.
  2. They has been playing.
    Show answerThey had been playing.: had.
  3. I had been knowing him.
    Show answerI had known him.: stative.
  4. He was tired since he has been working.
    Show answerHe was tired because he had been working.: past cause.

Say it another way

  1. “She waited 2 hours. Then the bus came.”
    Show answerShe had been waiting for two hours when the bus came.
  2. “They argued. They went quiet.”
    Show answerThey had been arguing before they went quiet.

Fill the gaps

When the doctor arrived, I (1) ___ (wait) for two hours. My eyes were sore because I (2) ___ (study) all night.

Show answers1 had been waiting · 2 had been studying

Speak and write

  • Explain why you were late last time using a cause with had been.
  • Retell a story where duration matters.

Making it stick

  • Keep a small notebook of your own mistakes. Write the wrong sentence, the right one, and why. Read it every other day.
  • Before you answer, ask: when did it happen, what do I mean, and what does the verb need?
  • Redo the quiz after a day, three days and a week.
  • Read examples aloud, then write three about your own life.
