Articles and nouns

A or an for one new thing, the for a known thing, and no article for general plurals and uncountables.

In plain words

Articles feel small but they carry a lot of meaning. Ask one question: does my reader already know which one I mean? If yes, use the. If not, and it is one countable thing, use a or an. If you are talking about a whole group or an idea in general, use no article. Nouns bring a second question: can I count it? That decides plural forms and whether a or an is allowed.

How to build it

A / an a before a consonant sound: a book, a university. an before a vowel sound: an hour, an apple.

The a specific or known thing: the book on the table, the sun.

No article general plurals and uncountables: Books help. Water is vital.

Plurals +s: cars · +es: boxes · y→ies: studies · irregular: men, children, people

When we use it

A or an: one of many, first mention

I saw a film last night.

The listener does not yet know which film.

The: known or unique

The film was excellent. The Internet changed daily life.

Second mention, or only one exists.

The: shared situation

Please close the door.

Both people know which door.

No article: general ideas

Education is important. Students need support.

General plural and uncountable nouns take no article.

The with superlatives and ordinals

the highest rate, the first step

Superlatives and first, second, last point to one specific thing.

Same noun, different article

I need a pen.
I need the pen I lent you.

New or any pen vs a known one.

She plays the piano.
She plays football.

Instruments take the. Sports do not.

He went to school.
He went to the school to meet the head.

Going for its purpose vs a particular building.

Life is hard.
The life of a farmer is hard.

General vs specific.

The choice depends on what the reader knows, not on the noun itself.

Mistakes people make

I have a information. → I have some information.

Information is uncountable: no a, no plural.

She gave me an advice. → She gave me some advice.

Advice is uncountable.

Internet is useful. → The Internet is useful.

There is only one Internet.

I am student. → I am a student.

A singular countable noun needs a, an, the or my.

Peoples are friendly. → People are friendly.

People is already plural.

The life is short. → Life is short.

A general idea takes no article.

An university → A university

The sound counts, not the letter. University starts with /j/.

Going deeper

Core test: which one?

Ask “which one?” in your head. If your reader can answer, use the. If not and it is one countable thing, use a or an. If you mean the whole class, use no article.

Countable and uncountable

Countable: book/books, idea/ideas. Uncountable: information, advice, research, equipment, furniture, traffic, knowledge, money, luggage, work. With uncountables use some, much, a lot of or a piece of: a piece of advice.

Nouns with two lives

Some nouns change meaning: paper (material) and a paper (an essay); experience (general) and an experience (an event); work and works. Check which meaning you want.

Sound, not spelling

an hour, an honest answer, an MBA. a university, a European country, a one-hour class. Use a or an by the first sound.

Names and places

Usually no article: Bangladesh, London, Mount Everest. With the: the United Kingdom, the Netherlands, the Himalayas, the Pacific, the Nile. Plural names, oceans, rivers and “of” names take the.

Plural spelling

Most: +s. After s, x, ch, sh, z: +es. Consonant + y: ies (city→cities). Vowel + y: +s (day→days). Irregular: man/men, child/children, person/people. Unchanged: sheep, fish. Greek and Latin: criterion/criteria, phenomenon/phenomena, analysis/analyses.

Shortcuts that work in exams

  • First time a/an, next time the. I bought a laptop. The laptop is fast.
  • Superlatives take the. the best, the most important.
  • Uncountable never takes a/an. some advice, not an advice.
  • General plural takes nothing. Children need sleep.

Where exams try to catch you

  • Society, education, technology: General ideas, no article: Technology has changed society.
  • “The government”: Usually with the. The young and the elderly also take the as a group.
  • Fixed phrases: in hospital (as a patient), at university, by bus, at home. But in the hospital (visiting).
  • Singular countable nouns: Never bare. Write “a child”, “the child” or “children”.

What to do, and what to leave alone

Do

  • Check every singular countable noun has a, an, the or my/this.
  • Use no article for general plural and uncountable nouns.
  • Use the for second mention.
  • Keep a list of uncountable nouns you often use.

Don’t

  • Don’t write “a information”, “an advice” or “a research”.
  • Don’t put the before general ideas: “the society”.
  • Don’t add -s to people or uncountables.
  • Don’t choose a or an by letter. Choose by sound.

Why we get it wrong

Mother-tongue transfer

Many languages, including Bengali, Hindi, Chinese and Russian, have no articles. “Known to the reader” is shown in another way.

Try this: Ask each time: does my reader know which one?

Counting differently

Words like advice and news may be countable in your language.

Try this: Keep a personal list of tricky uncountable nouns.

Speed

Articles are short, unstressed and easy to skip when you write fast.

Try this: Do one pass of your essay just for articles.

Small habits that help

  • Label every noun in a paragraph C (countable) or U (uncountable).
  • Notice how a and the are used in short English texts.
  • Collect uncountable surprises: advice, news, furniture, luggage.

In IELTS and real life

  • Speaking: say a and the clearly, and use some with uncountables.
  • Writing: general statements take no article (Technology improves life). Specific ones take the (The technology used in schools is…).
  • Reading: a vs the often shows new vs known information.
  • Listening: articles are weak sounds. Listen for a/an before singular countable nouns.

Real world: job applications and emails read much more naturally with correct articles.

Article errors rarely stop understanding, but they are among the most frequent errors in IELTS Writing, so they repay practice.

Practice

Spot the mistake

  1. I need an advice.
    Show answerI need some advice.: advice is uncountable.
  2. Internet has changed life.
    Show answerThe Internet has changed life.: there is only one.
  3. She is a honest girl.
    Show answerShe is an honest girl.: honest starts with a vowel sound.
  4. The education is important.
    Show answerEducation is important.: general idea, no article.

Say it another way

  1. Make it general: “The children need the sleep.”
    Show answerChildren need sleep.
  2. Fix the articles: “I bought laptop. Laptop is fast.”
    Show answerI bought a laptop. The laptop is fast.

Fill the gaps

I read (1) ___ article about (2) ___ education. (3) ___ article said that (4) ___ students need (5) ___ advice.

Show answers1 an · 2 (no article) · 3 The · 4 (no article) · 5 some

Speak and write

  • Describe your room: first mention with a or an, then use the.
  • Write six sentences using uncountable nouns: information, advice, research, furniture, news, luggage.

Making it stick

  • Each day, write three sentences and check every noun for its article.
  • Keep a small notebook of your own mistakes. Write the wrong sentence, the right one, and why. Read it every other day.
  • Redo the quiz after a day, three days and a week.
  • Read examples aloud, then write three about your own life.
