Subject–verb agreement

Refine agreement in formal writing, inversion, relative clauses and variable usage.

1. Agreement in complex subjects

In formal writing, identify the grammatical head of the subject. Long modifiers, prepositional phrases and relative clauses do not usually change its number.

The quality of the measurements collected across the three sites is inconsistent.
The measurements collected across the three sites are inconsistent.

2. Inversion and existential there

Inversion places the verb before the subject, especially after place expressions or negative adverbials. Agreement still follows the true subject.

On the edge of the field stands an old building.
Beyond the hills lie several villages.
Never have the results been replicated.
There are three explanations for the pattern.

3. Proximity agreement and formal editing

With either…or and neither…nor, proximity agreement is common. In highly edited prose, avoid awkward mismatches by restructuring the sentence.

Awkward: Neither the principal investigator nor the research assistants is available.
Clearer: The principal investigator is unavailable, and neither are the research assistants.

4. Notional agreement and collective nouns

Notional agreement follows the meaning rather than only the grammatical form. Collective nouns may take singular agreement when the group is a unit, or plural agreement when members are emphasised. Usage varies by dialect, so follow the style guide and remain consistent.

The committee has published its report. (committee as a body)
The committee are divided in their opinions. (members emphasised, especially British English)

5. Data, statistics and other variable cases

  • Data: traditionally plural in scientific and formal contexts (“the data show”), but singular use is common in general English (“the data is”). Follow the journal's style guide.
  • Statistics: singular for the academic subject (“Statistics is required”) and plural for numerical figures (“The statistics are alarming”).
  • None: may be singular or plural depending on meaning and context (“None of the evidence is conclusive”; “None of the participants were available”).
  • Majority, proportion, range: agreement depends on whether the phrase is treated as a single unit or whether the individuals/items are emphasised. Use the intended meaning and keep the sentence clear.

6. Titles, amounts and names

“Great Expectations” is a novel.
Ten thousand taka is a substantial sum.
The Netherlands is in Europe.
The findings of the study are relevant, but the evidence is limited.

7. Agreement in academic writing

Check the subject of each finite verb, not just the main verb. Embedded clauses may contain their own subject–verb pairs.

The proportion of districts that report outbreaks has increased. (main subject: proportion; relative-clause subject: that = districts)
The districts that report outbreaks have increased their surveillance. (main subject: districts)

8. Advanced editing checklist

  • Underline the head noun of every long subject.
  • Check each finite verb separately, including verbs inside relative clauses.
  • Check pronoun consistency with collective nouns.
  • Use the target journal's preferred style for data and collective nouns.
  • Rewrite sentences where proximity agreement makes the meaning hard to follow.

9. Advanced practice

2. Beyond the river ___ several villages.

3. In a traditional formal scientific style, the data ___ a strong association.

4. The lead author thanked the assistants who ___ the data.

5. None of the evidence ___ conclusive.
