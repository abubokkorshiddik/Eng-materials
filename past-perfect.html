Past perfect

Show that one past event happened before another past event or time.

earlier → laterpastnowfuture

In plain words

Sometimes a story needs one more step back. The past perfect is the before of the before: what had already happened when the main event took place.

How to build it

Positive had + past participle

Negative had not (hadn't) + past participle

Question Had + subject + past participle?

Key words before · after · by the time · already · never · until

When we use it

Earlier past event

The train had left before we reached the station.

Leaving came first.

Cause of a past state

She was tired because she had slept badly.

The earlier event explains.

Reported speech

He said he had finished.

Shift back from “I finished/have finished”.

Third conditional

If I had studied, I would have passed.

Unreal past.

Tenses that get mixed up with it

When I arrived, she left.
When I arrived, she had left.

Left after vs before arrival.

I finished and went home.
I had finished before I went home.

Order stressed.

I saw him yesterday.
I had seen him before that trip.

One past point vs two.

Time words are clues, not rules. The meaning you want decides the tense.

Mistakes people make

The film already started when we came. → The film had already started when we came.

Earlier event needs past perfect.

She had went. → She had gone.

Past participle.

After he had ate… → After he had eaten…

eat → eaten.

I had finished yesterday at 5. → I finished yesterday at 5.

One event, no second past point.

Going deeper

Core idea: the earlier of two past points

You need two past points. The past perfect marks the earlier: “When I arrived, the film had started.” Without a second point, use past simple.

Where it is required

Reported speech (“He said he had finished”), third conditional (“If I had studied, I would have passed”), wish about the past (“I wish I had gone”), by the time + past (“By the time we arrived, it had ended”).

Sequence is not always needed

If words like before/after already show order, past simple is fine: “After I finished, I left.” Past perfect adds clarity or cause.

Hadn't and spoken forms

I'd, he'd, hadn't. Beware: he'd = he had / he would. Listen to the next word: “He'd finished” (had) vs “He'd finish” (would).

Shortcuts that work in exams

  • By the time + past → perfect main clause. By the time he came, I had left.
  • Already/just/never in past stories. Often perfect.
  • Because + earlier cause. He was angry because he had lost.
  • Check order first. Draw two dots on a line.

Where exams try to catch you

  • “After he finished” vs “had finished”: Both ok.
  • Single event with a date: I finished at 5 (not had finished).
  • Reported speech: she said she finished: Better: she had finished (formal).

What to do, and what to leave alone

Do

  • Draw two points on a line before choosing.
  • Use had + participle only for the earlier event.
  • Use it with by the time, already, never, because.
  • Use it in reported speech.

Don't

  • Don't use it with only one past event.
  • Don't use it everywhere to sound advanced.
  • Don't use had went/ate/seen incorrectly.
  • Don't mix up he'd (had/would).

Why we get it wrong

No second time point

“I had finished yesterday”.

Try this: Remove had or add a second point.

Same tense for both events

When I came, he already left.

Try this: The earlier one gets had.

Participle errors

had ate, had went.

Try this: Learn eaten, gone.

Small habits that help

  • Retell your morning with 'before I left, I had…'.
  • Underline first and second events in reading.
  • In Listening, 'd + participle: listen for had.

In IELTS and real life

  • Speaking: “I had never travelled abroad before that trip.”
  • Writing: clarify order in historical accounts.
  • Reading: decide which event happened first.
  • Listening: hear had/'d + participle.

Real world: explain what had already happened when you arrived.

TOEFL, PTE, GRE and bank or civil-service English tests ask the same thing: does the tense match the time you mean?

Practice

Spot the mistake

  1. When I arrived, he already left.
    Show answer…he had already left.: earlier event.
  2. She had went.
    Show answerShe had gone.: participle.
  3. I had seen him yesterday.
    Show answerI saw him yesterday.: one point.
  4. After he had ate, he left.
    Show answerAfter he had eaten, he left.: participle.

Say it another way

  1. “I never saw snow. Then I travelled.”
    Show answerI had never seen snow before I travelled.
  2. “The film began. We arrived.” (by the time)
    Show answerBy the time we arrived, the film had begun.

Fill the gaps

When I (1) ___ (reach) the station, the train (2) ___ (already/leave). I realised I (3) ___ (forget) my ticket at home, so I (4) ___ (go) back.

Show answers1 reached · 2 had already left · 3 had forgotten · 4 went

Speak and write

  • Tell a story in which an earlier event explains a later result.
  • Complete five sentences: By the time I arrived…, I had…

Making it stick

  • Keep a small notebook of your own mistakes. Write the wrong sentence, the right one, and why. Read it every other day.
  • Before you answer, ask: when did it happen, what do I mean, and what does the verb need?
  • Redo the quiz after a day, three days and a week.
  • Read examples aloud, then write three about your own life.
