Future perfect

Something will be completed before a future time or event.

done → deadlinepastnowfuture

In plain words

Fast-forward to a deadline and look back. Will the job be done by then? If yes, you want the future perfect.

How to build it

Positive will have + past participle

Negative will not have + past participle

Question Will + subject + have + past participle?

Key words by Friday · by then · by the time · by the end of

When we use it

Done before a deadline

I will have finished the report by Friday.

Complete no later than Friday.

Predicted totals

By June she will have published three papers.

Expected quantity.

Looking back from the future

In ten years the city will have changed.

Imagined viewpoint.

Targets

They will have built the bridge by 2030.

Projected completion.

Tenses that get mixed up with it

I will finish by Friday.
I will have finished by Friday.

Both fine; perfect stresses completion.

At 8 I will work.
At 8 I will be working.

Simple vs in progress.

By next year I will have worked here 10 years.
…will have been working…

Period vs ongoing activity.

Time words are clues, not rules. The meaning you want decides the tense.

Mistakes people make

I will have finish. → I will have finished.

Participle.

She will has completed. → She will have completed.

have after will.

By the time he comes, I will finish. → …I will have finished.

Completion before.

I will have been finish. → I will have finished.

No been.

Going deeper

Core idea: look back from the future

Jump to a future deadline and look back: “By Friday I will have finished.” Focus is completion at or before that point.

By vs until

By = deadline, not later than: “I will have finished by 5.” Until = continues to that time: “I will work until 5.” This pair is a classic exam trap.

Duration with stative verbs

“By next year we will have lived here for ten years.” With activity verbs, continuous may stress duration: “will have been living”.

Time clause rule

“By the time you arrive, I will have cooked.” After by the time/when/before use present simple, not will.

Shortcuts that work in exams

  • By + future time. → future perfect.
  • Check completion. If it means finished, perfect.
  • Present in time clause. by the time she arrives.
  • Quantities. By June she will have written 3 articles.

Where exams try to catch you

  • “By 5 I will be working”: Possible, but not completion.
  • “Until” vs “by”: Different meaning.
  • “Will have” + base verb: No: will have done.

What to do, and what to leave alone

Do

  • Use it for completion by a deadline.
  • Use by to show the deadline.
  • Use present simple after by the time.
  • Use it with quantities or targets.

Don't

  • Don't write will have + base verb: will have finish.
  • Don't use it for duration of activity: use perfect continuous.
  • Don't use until for completion.
  • Don't use will in by the time clauses.

Why we get it wrong

by/until confusion

I will finish until Friday.

Try this: by = deadline, until = continues.

Overuse

Every future as will have.

Try this: Use only for completion.

Participle

will have ate.

Try this: will have eaten.

Small habits that help

  • Write five goals: 'By December I will have…'.
  • In Task 1 forecasts, use it with 'by 2040'.
  • In Listening, schedules: 'will have been finished'.

In IELTS and real life

  • Speaking: “By the end of the year I will have completed my IELTS preparation.”
  • Writing: forecasts and deadlines.
  • Reading: actions complete before a future point.
  • Listening: will have + participle in schedules.

Real world: project updates — “By Monday we will have tested everything.”

TOEFL, PTE, GRE and bank or civil-service English tests ask the same thing: does the tense match the time you mean?

Practice

Spot the mistake

  1. I will have finish by 5.
    Show answerI will have finished by 5.: participle.
  2. By the time he will arrive, I will have left.
    Show answerBy the time he arrives, I will have left.: time clause.
  3. She will has done.
    Show answerShe will have done.: have.
  4. I will work by Friday.
    Show answerI will have finished by Friday.: by = completion.

Say it another way

  1. “I will finish at 5 or earlier.”
    Show answerI will have finished by 5.
  2. “By 2030 … built (bridge)”
    Show answerBy 2030 they will have built the bridge.

Fill the gaps

By the time you read this, I (1) ___ (leave). By Friday we (2) ___ (complete) the report. By 2030 the city (3) ___ (build) 50 new schools.

Show answers1 will have left · 2 will have completed · 3 will have built

Speak and write

  • List five goals and write them as 'By the end of… I will have…'.
  • Write a 100-word forecast for 2035 using future perfect twice.

Making it stick

  • Keep a small notebook of your own mistakes. Write the wrong sentence, the right one, and why. Read it every other day.
  • Before you answer, ask: when did it happen, what do I mean, and what does the verb need?
  • Redo the quiz after a day, three days and a week.
  • Read examples aloud, then write three about your own life.
