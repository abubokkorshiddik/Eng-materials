Beginner level

Past simple

Use the past simple for finished events, past states and past habits at a finished time.

finished timepastnowfuture

In plain words

Something happened, it is over, and you know (or can say) when. That is the past simple. It is the tense of stories, news, and the history of your own life.

How to build it

Positive Subject + past form: worked, went

Negative did not + base verb

Question Did + subject + base verb?

Be was / were (no did)

When we use it

Finished event

I submitted the form yesterday.

Yesterday is a finished time.

Sequence of events

She opened the file, checked it and saved it.

Story and process order.

Past habit or state

We walked to school. He lived in Dhaka for ten years.

A period that is over.

Historical data (IELTS Task 1)

Sales rose from 20% in 2010 to 35% in 2015.

Finished years: past simple.

Tenses that get mixed up with it

I saw her yesterday.
I have seen her.

Finished time vs experience/result now.

She worked there for 5 years.
She has worked there for 5 years.

Finished period vs still true.

I cooked dinner.
I was cooking dinner.

Completed vs in progress.

Time words are clues, not rules. The meaning you want decides the tense.

Mistakes people make

She didn't went. → She didn't go.

Did already shows the past.

Did you saw it? → Did you see it?

Base verb after did.

I have seen him yesterday. → I saw him yesterday.

Finished time: past simple.

Where you went? → Where did you go?

Questions need did.

Going deeper

Core test: is the time closed?

Past simple fits when the event is placed in a finished time, stated or understood: “I met her in 2019”, “Did you sleep well?” If the period reaches now, think present perfect.

did/didn't: one past marker only

After did/didn't/did…? the verb is base: Did you see? She didn't go. Be uses was/were without did. Subject questions have no did: Who called you? vs Who did you call?

Used to and would

“I used to play outside” (past habit/state). “Would” only for repeated actions, not states: “I would walk to school” ok; “I would know him” not ok.

-ed sounds and spelling

/t/ worked, /d/ played, /ɪd/ wanted. study→studied, stop→stopped, play→played. Learn irregular verbs in groups: bring/brought/buy/bought/think/thought; sing/sang/ring/rang.

Shortcuts that work in exams

  • Past markers. ago, yesterday, last, in + year, when I was → past simple.
  • Sequence words. first, then, after that = past simple chain.
  • Did + go. Never “did went”. One mark only.
  • Chart years. Years all finished → past simple in Task 1.

Where exams try to catch you

  • “Yesterday I have…”: Wrong: closed time.
  • “Did you …” short answers: Yes, I did (not “Yes, I did went”).
  • Questions with be: Was she tired? not Did she be tired?

What to do, and what to leave alone

Do

  • Mark every sentence with its time: finished or not?
  • Use did + base verb for ordinary questions and negatives.
  • Learn irregular verbs in rhyming groups.
  • Keep one tense through a finished story, then shift only for a new time.

Don't

  • Don't put two past markers: did went, didn't saw.
  • Don't use present perfect with yesterday, ago, last week.
  • Don't add -ed to irregular verbs: goed, buyed.
  • Don't switch between past and present in one story.

Why we get it wrong

Over-correction

You know did marks the past, so you also change the verb.

Try this: Memorise: after did, the verb returns to base.

Irregular verbs not automatic

goed, runned.

Try this: Revise 5 verbs a day with sentences.

Present perfect from your first language

“I have seen him yesterday”.

Try this: Closed time word = past simple.

Small habits that help

  • Retell yesterday in 6 sentences every day for a week.
  • Check every Task 1 verb for the right year/time.
  • When listening, note the final -ed sound: it is often swallowed.

In IELTS and real life

  • Speaking Part 1/2: “I enjoyed science because…” — tell stories in the past simple.
  • Writing Task 1: use it for data in finished years (2000–2020).
  • Reading: dates and “in 2019” point to completed events.
  • Listening: catch irregular forms (went, took, found) and corrections.

Real world: tell a friend what you did last weekend.

TOEFL, PTE, GRE and bank or civil-service English tests ask the same thing: does the tense match the time you mean?

Practice

Spot the mistake

  1. I didn't saw him.
    Show answerI didn't see him.: base after didn't.
  2. He goed home.
    Show answerHe went home.: irregular.
  3. Where you went?
    Show answerWhere did you go?: auxiliary.
  4. Did she was late?
    Show answerWas she late?: be.

Say it another way

  1. “He started at 5 and finished at 7.” (past)
    Show answerHe worked from 5 to 7.
  2. “Last year, my visit to Rome.” (visit)
    Show answerI visited Rome last year.

Fill the gaps

Last summer I (1) ___ (go) to Cox's Bazar. We (2) ___ (leave) early, (3) ___ (arrive) at noon and (4) ___ (not/swim) because the sea (5) ___ (be) rough.

Show answers1 went · 2 left · 3 arrived · 4 didn't swim · 5 was

Speak and write

  • Tell a 2-minute story about a trip using at least six past simple verbs.
  • Describe a chart (2005–2015) in 80 words using rose, fell, remained, peaked.

Making it stick

  • Keep a small notebook of your own mistakes. Write the wrong sentence, the right one, and why. Read it every other day.
  • Before you answer, ask: when did it happen, what do I mean, and what does the verb need?
  • Redo the quiz after a day, three days and a week.
  • Read examples aloud, then write three about your own life.
