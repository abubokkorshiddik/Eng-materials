Future continuous

An activity will be in progress at a future moment, or an expected future event.

in progress laterpastnowfuture

In plain words

Jump forward to tomorrow at 9 a.m. and look around. What is going on? That is the future continuous: a picture of the future, not a decision.

How to build it

Positive will be + verb-ing

Negative will not be + verb-ing

Question Will + subject + be + verb-ing?

Key words this time tomorrow · at 8 next Monday · while

When we use it

In progress then

At 9 tomorrow I will be taking my exam.

Underway at that time.

Expected events

This time next week we will be travelling.

Part of the expected future.

Polite questions

Will you be using the computer this afternoon?

Soft way to ask plans.

Parallel activities

While I am studying, my brother will be working.

Same future period.

Tenses that get mixed up with it

I will finish at 5.
I will be finishing at 5.

Completion vs in progress.

Will you use the car?
Will you be using the car?

Direct vs polite inquiry.

I will know.
NOT I will be knowing.

Stative: simple.

Time words are clues, not rules. The meaning you want decides the tense.

Mistakes people make

I will working at 8. → I will be working at 8.

will be + -ing.

She will be work. → She will be working.

-ing form.

He will be knowing soon. → He will know soon.

Stative.

Will you be use it? → Will you be using it?

-ing.

Going deeper

Core idea: look from a future moment

Stand at a future time and see an activity in progress: “At 9 tomorrow I will be sitting the exam.” You are not announcing a decision, you are describing a scene.

Polite enquiry

“Will you be using the car tonight?” asks about someone's plan without pressure. “Will you use…” can sound like a request.

Future continuous vs will

“I will call you at 8” (decision). “I will be waiting for your call at 8” (state at that time). “Will you be seeing Ali?” (is it already your plan?)

Not for stative verbs

know, like, need, own, belong stay simple: “I will know the result tomorrow.”

Shortcuts that work in exams

  • This time tomorrow. = future continuous.
  • Polite question. Will you be + -ing?
  • Two parallel future actions. While… will be…
  • Stative check. If the verb is a state, use simple.

Where exams try to catch you

  • “At 5 I will finish” vs “be finishing”: Different meaning.
  • “By 5” with continuous: By = deadline → future perfect.
  • Present simple in clauses: “When I arrive, she will be waiting.” (not will arrive)

What to do, and what to leave alone

Do

  • Use it for what will be in progress at a future time.
  • Use it for polite questions: Will you be using…?
  • Use stative verbs in simple.
  • Check be + -ing both present.

Don't

  • Don't use it for a decision: use will.
  • Don't use by + continuous: by = future perfect.
  • Don't forget be: will working.
  • Don't use will after when in time clauses.

Why we get it wrong

Confusing with will

I will study at 8 vs I will be studying at 8.

Try this: Ask: decision or scene?

Dropping be

will working, will go to be.

Try this: Say will be, then add -ing.

Ignoring stative verbs

will be knowing.

Try this: Know, want, own stay simple.

Small habits that help

  • Write where you will be at 8 tomorrow, 8 next Monday, 8 next month.
  • Use it in Part 2 for dreams: 'This time next year I will be…'.
  • In Listening, will be + -ing often signals a schedule.

In IELTS and real life

  • Speaking: “This time next year I will be studying for my master's.”
  • Writing: rare; only for future activity in progress.
  • Reading: spot an activity in progress at a future point.
  • Listening: will be + -ing vs will + base.

Real world: polite questions and schedules — “I'll be driving at 7, so call after 8.”

TOEFL, PTE, GRE and bank or civil-service English tests ask the same thing: does the tense match the time you mean?

Practice

Spot the mistake

  1. I will working.
    Show answerI will be working.: be needed.
  2. She will be to travel.
    Show answerShe will be travelling.: -ing.
  3. I will be knowing.
    Show answerI will know.: stative.
  4. Will you be use it?
    Show answerWill you be using it?: -ing.

Say it another way

  1. “Rain stops at 6. We drive then.” (continuous)
    Show answerWe will be driving at 6.
  2. “Ask politely: Will you use the lab?”
    Show answerWill you be using the lab?

Fill the gaps

At 7 p.m. tomorrow I (1) ___ (fly) to Dubai, so I (2) ___ (not/use) my phone. My friends (3) ___ (wait) for me when I (4) ___ (land).

Show answers1 will be flying · 2 won't be using · 3 will be waiting · 4 land

Speak and write

  • Describe where you will be and what you will be doing this time next year.
  • Write five polite questions with Will you be…?

Making it stick

  • Keep a small notebook of your own mistakes. Write the wrong sentence, the right one, and why. Read it every other day.
  • Before you answer, ask: when did it happen, what do I mean, and what does the verb need?
  • Redo the quiz after a day, three days and a week.
  • Read examples aloud, then write three about your own life.
