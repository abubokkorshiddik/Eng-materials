Present simple and present continuous

Present simple = facts, habits, schedules. Present continuous = happening now, temporary, changing.

habit & nowpastnowfuture

In plain words

Think of two cameras. One takes a wide shot: what is true, and what you do again and again. The other zooms in on right now. Before you write a verb, ask yourself: is this always, or is this happening now? That one question gets you most of the way.

How to build it

Simple + I/you/we/they work · he/she/it works

Simple − do/does not + base verb

Simple ? Do/Does + subject + base verb?

Continuous am/is/are (not) + verb-ing

When we use it

Facts and habits (simple)

Water boils at 100°C. I study every evening.

Use for what is generally true or regular.

Timetables (simple)

The exam starts at 10 a.m.

Fixed schedules use the simple, even for the future.

Now and around now (continuous)

I am preparing for IELTS this month.

Activity in progress in the current period.

Changes and plans (continuous)

Prices are rising. I am meeting her tomorrow.

Developing trends and arranged plans.

Tenses that get mixed up with it

I usually walk to work.
I am taking the bus this week.

Routine vs temporary change.

She teaches English.
She is teaching while Ms Lee is away.

Regular role vs temporary arrangement.

The train leaves at 8.
I am leaving at 8.

Timetable vs personal arrangement.

Time words are clues, not rules. The meaning you want decides the tense.

Mistakes people make

She work every day. → She works every day.

Add -s for he/she/it.

He doesn't likes tea. → He doesn't like tea.

After does/doesn't use the base verb.

I am knowing him. → I know him.

Know is stative: no continuous.

I live here since 2020. → I have lived here since 2020.

Since + period up to now needs present perfect.

Going deeper

Core test: pattern or moment?

Ask: is this a pattern (habit, fact, schedule) or a moment/period in progress? Pattern → simple. In progress, temporary or changing → continuous. “I live in Dhaka” is stable; “I am living in Dhaka this year” is temporary.

Stative verbs and double meanings

Know, believe, understand, want, need, prefer, own, belong, seem are normally simple. Some switch meaning: I think it is good (opinion) vs I am thinking about it (process); She has a car vs She is having lunch; He is rude vs He is being rude (behaving so now).

Present tenses for the future

Timetable → simple: “The bus leaves at 8.” Personal arrangement → continuous: “I am meeting her at 8.” After when/before/until/as soon as, use simple for the future: “I will call when I arrive.”

Spelling and agreement

he/she/it + s/es (goes, watches, studies). -ing: write→writing, run→running, lie→lying. Subjects: “The number of students is rising”, “A number of students are rising/are choosing…”.

Shortcuts that work in exams

  • Always/usually/every = simple. Frequency words point to a pattern.
  • Now/at the moment/currently = continuous. But never with stative verbs.
  • Is it a graph or table?. “The chart shows…” (simple) not “is showing”.
  • Annoying habit. always + continuous = complaint: “He is always losing keys.”

Where exams try to catch you

  • “These days / nowadays”: Both tenses; trend → continuous, general fact → simple.
  • “Still”: Either tense; check meaning.
  • “I am agreeing”: Wrong. Agree is a verb state: “I agree.”

What to do, and what to leave alone

Do

  • Ask first: habit/fact or happening now?
  • Add -s/-es for he, she, it, and check the subject (people = plural).
  • Use simple for chart descriptions and schedules.
  • Keep stative verbs simple: know, want, need, believe.

Don't

  • Don't write “I am knowing/wanting/agreeing”.
  • Don't drop am/is/are: “She studying”.
  • Don't use does + verb-s: “does she works”.
  • Don't use now-words with simple for a current action.

Why we get it wrong

Mother-tongue transfer

Many languages have no -s or no be + -ing, so learners skip them.

Try this: Say the pattern aloud: she works, she is working.

Reading words, not meaning

“now” makes you choose continuous even for facts.

Try this: Decide pattern vs moment before reading the time word.

Fast writing

-s forgotten under time pressure.

Try this: Do a 30-second -s/agreement check in Task 2.

Small habits that help

  • Underline every verb in your last essay and label simple/continuous.
  • In Speaking, answer routine questions in simple and 'at the moment' questions in continuous.
  • In Listening, the -ing ending is weak: listen for am/is/are.

In IELTS and real life

  • Speaking: use simple for routines and opinions, continuous for current projects: “At the moment I am preparing for IELTS.”
  • Writing: “The chart shows…” (simple) and “More people are choosing online courses” (trend).
  • Reading: spot general facts (simple) versus research in progress (are investigating).
  • Listening: catch -s endings, am/is/are + -ing, and time words like “at the moment”.

Real world: describe your job (simple) and what you are working on this week (continuous).

TOEFL, PTE, GRE and bank or civil-service English tests ask the same thing: does the tense match the time you mean?

Practice

Spot the mistake

  1. She go to school daily.
    Show answerShe goes to school daily.: add -s.
  2. I am having a car.
    Show answerI have a car.: possession is stative.
  3. The sun is rising in the east.
    Show answerThe sun rises in the east.: fact.
  4. Look! It rains.
    Show answerLook! It is raining.: visible now.

Say it another way

  1. “I plan to leave at 6 (arranged).” (continuous)
    Show answerI am leaving at 6.
  2. “Train time: 6 a.m.” (simple)
    Show answerThe train leaves at 6 a.m.

Fill the gaps

Every morning I (1) ___ (wake) at six. Today I (2) ___ (stay) in bed because I (3) ___ (feel) ill. My sister usually (4) ___ (cook), but this week she (5) ___ (visit) friends.

Show answers1 wake · 2 am staying · 3 feel · 4 cooks · 5 is visiting

Speak and write

  • Speak 1 minute about your normal day, then 1 minute about what is different this week.
  • Write six sentences: two facts, two habits, two temporary situations.

Making it stick

  • Keep a small notebook of your own mistakes. Write the wrong sentence, the right one, and why. Read it every other day.
  • Before you answer, ask: when did it happen, what do I mean, and what does the verb need?
  • Redo the quiz after a day, three days and a week.
  • Read examples aloud, then write three about your own life.
