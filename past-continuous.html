Past continuous

Use it for an activity in progress at a past moment, or as background to a past event.

in progress thenpastnowfuture

In plain words

Imagine pausing a video of last night. The past continuous tells us what was going on inside that frame. It sets the scene. The past simple then brings in the action.

How to build it

Positive was/were + verb-ing

Negative was/were not + verb-ing

Question Was/Were + subject + verb-ing?

Spelling write→writing · run→running · lie→lying

When we use it

In progress at a past time

At 9 p.m. I was studying.

Look back at one moment.

Interrupted action

I was walking home when it started to rain.

Background (continuous) + event (simple).

Two actions together

While I was reading, he was cooking.

Overlapping activities.

Scene setting

The rain was falling and people were hurrying home.

Backdrop for a story.

Tenses that get mixed up with it

I read the report.
I was reading the report at 8.

Completed vs in progress.

He always interrupted me.
He was always interrupting me.

Neutral habit vs irritation.

I knew the answer.
I was thinking about it.

Stative vs dynamic meaning.

Time words are clues, not rules. The meaning you want decides the tense.

Mistakes people make

They was waiting. → They were waiting.

They takes were.

She didn't studying. → She wasn't studying.

Use was/were + not.

Did you were sleeping? → Were you sleeping?

No did with was/were.

I was knowing him. → I knew him.

Know is stative.

Going deeper

Core idea: a frame around a moment

Picture a photograph of a past moment. The past continuous describes what was in progress inside it. “At 8 I was cooking” says nothing about when cooking began or ended.

When vs while

While usually introduces the ongoing action. When can introduce an event: “I was cooking when she called.” Two ongoing actions: “While I was cooking, she was reading.” Sequences use past simple: “When I arrived, she left.”

Past continuous vs past simple: completed or not?

“I wrote the essay last night” (finished). “I was writing the essay last night” (activity; unknown if finished). “At 8 she was crossing the road” (stat) vs “She crossed the road” (action done).

Polite and soft uses

“I was wondering if you could help” is more polite than “I wonder…”. “I was hoping to speak to you.”

Shortcuts that work in exams

  • Interruption pattern. Long action = continuous; short event = simple.
  • while = continuous. Most of the time.
  • No stative verbs. knew not was knowing.
  • Scene-setting in Part 2. Start with 2 continuous background sentences.

Where exams try to catch you

  • “When” with continuous: “When I was cooking, he called” is possible but the meaning shifts to period; decide by meaning.
  • “All day” + simple: “I studied all day” is fine; continuous stresses process.
  • Sequence confusion: “When I was arriving…” is odd: arrive is momentary.

What to do, and what to leave alone

Do

  • Use it for background: what was in progress.
  • Use past simple for the event that interrupts.
  • Use while with two ongoing actions.
  • Check was vs were.

Don't

  • Don't use the continuous for a finished story sequence.
  • Don't use stative verbs: was knowing, was liking.
  • Don't write did + was.
  • Don't overuse it to sound advanced.

Why we get it wrong

Mixing background and main events

Everything in continuous: “I was opening the door, I was seeing…”.

Try this: Keep main events in simple.

Confusing when/while

“While the phone rang…” (short event).

Try this: While + ongoing, when + event.

Agreement slips

they was, I were.

Try this: Memorise I/he/she/it was; you/we/they were.

Small habits that help

  • In Part 2, open with two scene-setting continuous sentences.
  • Draw the interrupted action as a long line crossed by a short line.
  • In Reading, find the continuous = what was happening in the background.

In IELTS and real life

  • Speaking Part 2: set the scene (“It was raining and I was travelling home…”), then use past simple for events.
  • Writing: use rarely, e.g. “while authorities were expanding transport…”.
  • Reading: find what was already happening when another event occurred.
  • Listening: hear was/were + -ing in quick speech.

Real world: explain an alibi or a story: “What were you doing when it happened?”

TOEFL, PTE, GRE and bank or civil-service English tests ask the same thing: does the tense match the time you mean?

Practice

Spot the mistake

  1. I was go to school.
    Show answerI was going to school.: -ing.
  2. She didn't studying.
    Show answerShe wasn't studying.: negative.
  3. While I walked, it was raining.
    Show answerWhile I was walking, it rained.: background vs event.
  4. They was playing.
    Show answerThey were playing.: agreement.

Say it another way

  1. “Rain began. I walked home.” (when)
    Show answerI was walking home when it began to rain.
  2. “She read. He cooked.” (while)
    Show answerWhile she was reading, he was cooking.

Fill the gaps

It (1) ___ (rain) and people (2) ___ (hurry) home. I (3) ___ (wait) for a bus when a car (4) ___ (stop) beside me.

Show answers1 was raining · 2 were hurrying · 3 was waiting · 4 stopped

Speak and write

  • Tell the story of an unexpected event: two background sentences, then events.
  • Write what you and your family were doing at 8 p.m. yesterday.

Making it stick

  • Keep a small notebook of your own mistakes. Write the wrong sentence, the right one, and why. Read it every other day.
  • Before you answer, ask: when did it happen, what do I mean, and what does the verb need?
  • Redo the quiz after a day, three days and a week.
  • Read examples aloud, then write three about your own life.
