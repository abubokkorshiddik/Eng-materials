Subject–verb agreement

Learn the essential rule: the verb must agree with its subject in number and person.

1. What is subject–verb agreement?

The subject tells us who or what a sentence is about. The verb tells us what the subject does or what state it is in. In the present simple, a third-person singular subject usually takes a verb ending in -s or -es.

Singular subject

She works every day.

The subject she is singular, so the verb is works.

Plural subject

They work every day.

The subject they is plural, so the verb is work.

2. Present simple: the basic rule

I / You / We / They + base verb

I study English. They live in Bangladesh.

He / She / It + verb-s or verb-es

He studies English. She watches television.

Examples

  • My brother plays football.
  • My friends play football.
  • The student reads every evening.
  • The students read every evening.

Remember: In affirmative present-simple sentences, add -s or -es to the verb with he, she, it, or a singular noun.

3. The verb “be”

Am

I am ready.

Is

She is ready.

Are

They are ready.

Past forms

He was tired. They were tired.

Use am with I, is with he/she/it and singular nouns, and are with you/we/they and plural nouns. In the past, use was with I/he/she/it and were with you/we/they.

4. Negative sentences and questions

After does or doesn't, use the base form of the main verb.

She doesn't like coffee.

Correct: doesn't + like.

Does he work here?

Correct: does + work.

Incorrect: She doesn't likes coffee.

Why? The third-person singular ending is already carried by “does”. Do not add another -s to the main verb.

5. Common beginner mistakes

Incorrect: He go to school.

Correct: He goes to school.

Incorrect: They goes to school.

Correct: They go to school.

Incorrect: She don't understand.

Correct: She doesn't understand.

Incorrect: The students is ready.

Correct: The students are ready.
