In plain words

Every tense answers two questions. When? Past, present or future. What shape is the action? Choose the shape first, then the time, and the tense name follows.

Simple
The whole fact, habit or event.

Continuous
An action in progress at a particular time.

Perfect
An action completed before, or connected to, a reference time.

Perfect continuous
The duration of an action up to a reference time.

Present tenses

Present simple

Subject + base verb; he/she/it + verb-s

Example: She works every day.

Use it for facts, habits and regular activities.

Clues: always, usually, every day, on Mondays

Present continuous

am/is/are + verb-ing

Example: I am studying now.

Use it for actions happening now, temporary situations and arranged plans.

Clues: now, at the moment, currently, this week

Present perfect

have/has + past participle

Example: I have finished my homework.

Use it for experience, present results and unfinished periods.

Clues: already, just, yet, ever, never, since, for

have/has been + verb-ing

Use it to stress duration up to now or a recent activity.

Clues: for, since, all day, lately, recently

Past tenses

Past simple

Past form of the verb

Example: I visited Rome in 2019.

Use it for completed events at a finished time.

Clues: yesterday, last week, ago, in 2019

Past continuous

was/were + verb-ing

Example: I was cooking when you called.

Use it for an action in progress at a past moment.

Clues: while, when, at 8 last night

Past perfect

had + past participle

Use it to make the order of two past events clear.

Future tenses

Future simple

will + base verb

Example: I will help you.

Use it for predictions, promises and decisions made at the time of speaking.

Clues: tomorrow, probably, I think, soon

Future continuous

will be + verb-ing

Example: This time tomorrow I will be flying.

Use it for an action that will be in progress at a future moment.

Clues: this time tomorrow, at 8 tonight

Future perfect

Example: By June I will have graduated.

Quick way to choose

  • Is it a habit or a fact? Present simple.
  • Is it happening now or around now? Present continuous.
  • Did it finish at a stated past time? Past simple.
  • Is a past event connected to now? Present perfect.
  • Were two events in the past? Use past perfect for the earlier event when you need to make the order clear.
  • Do you want to stress duration? Consider a perfect continuous tense.
  • Will something be completed before a future deadline? Future perfect.
