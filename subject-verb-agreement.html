Subject–verb agreement

The verb must match its real subject, not the noun nearest to it.

In plain words

Agreement is a handshake between the subject and the verb. A singular subject takes a singular verb, a plural subject takes a plural verb. It sounds easy until the subject is hidden behind a long phrase, or a word like people, news or a number of looks like one thing but acts like another. The fix is always the same: find the real subject, decide singular or plural, then choose the verb.

How to build it

Singular one thing: is, was, has, does, works

Plural more than one: are, were, have, do, work

Present he/she/it + verb-s: The graph shows. The graphs show.

Shrink it Replace the subject with he, she, it or they, then pick the verb.

When we use it

The basic match

The student works hard. The students work hard.

Singular third person takes -s in the present.

Phrases between subject and verb

The cost of new buildings has risen.

“Of new buildings” is extra detail. The subject is cost.

Two subjects joined by and

Reading and writing are tested in IELTS.

Two things joined by and take a plural verb.

Or, nor, either…or, neither…nor

Neither the teacher nor the students were ready.

The verb agrees with the nearer subject.

There is / there are

There are three reasons for this.

The real subject comes after there.

Groups

The committee has made its decision. The police are investigating.

Most group nouns act as one unit. Police, people and children are always plural.

Easy to confuse

Everyone is ready.
All of them are ready.

Everyone, everybody, someone, no one and each are singular.

The news is good.
The results are good.

News, economics, physics and mathematics end in -s but are singular.

The number of students is rising.
A number of students are choosing this.

The number of = singular. A number of = several = plural.

The police are here.
The policeman is here.

Police is always plural.

When in doubt, shrink the subject: replace it with he, she, it or they and see which verb fits.

Mistakes people make

People is worried about prices. → People are worried about prices.

People is always plural.

The number of cars are increasing. → The number of cars is increasing.

Number is the subject, and it is singular.

A lot of people was present. → A lot of people were present.

Follow the noun after “a lot of”: people.

Everyone have a phone. → Everyone has a phone.

Everyone is singular.

The results of the study shows the problem. → The results of the study show the problem.

Results is the subject, not study.

There is many problems. → There are many problems.

Check the noun that comes after there.

Each of the students are late. → Each of the students is late.

Each is singular.

The information are useful. → The information is useful.

Uncountable nouns take singular verbs.

Going deeper

Core test: find the head noun

Cross out any phrase beginning with of, in, with, along with, as well as or together with, and anything between commas. What is left is the subject. “The manager, along with her team, is attending.”

Uncountable nouns

Information, advice, research, equipment, furniture, evidence, knowledge, progress, traffic and news are uncountable and take singular verbs: The research is clear. Do not add -s to them.

Quantity words

Singular: each, every, either, neither, one of, the number of. Plural: both, several, many, few, a number of, a couple of. Agrees with the noun after: all of, most of, some of, half of, a lot of. Most of the water is clean. Most of the students are happy.

None of

“None of the students was” and “none of the students were” are both used. Formal writing often prefers singular, everyday English plural. Pick one and be consistent.

After who, which and that

The verb agrees with the noun the pronoun points to. “She is one of the students who work late” (who = the students). “She is the only student who works late.”

Titles and subjects of study

The United States is large. Mathematics is difficult. Names of books and films are singular: Friends was popular.

Shortcuts that work in exams

  • Shrink the subject. He, she, it → -s, is, has. They → no -s, are, have.
  • Look for “of”. In “the X of Y”, X is usually the subject.
  • People, police, children, men, women. Always plural.
  • Uncountable = singular. Information, advice, research, news.

Where exams try to catch you

  • “A number of” and “the number of”: The most tested pair in IELTS Writing Task 1.
  • “Neither of” and “either of”: Formally singular: Neither of them was late.
  • “One of the…”: One of the reasons is… One is the subject.
  • Inverted order: Beside the house stand two trees. The verb still agrees with trees.

What to do, and what to leave alone

Do

  • Find the subject before choosing the verb.
  • Treat people, police and children as plural.
  • Treat uncountable nouns as singular.
  • Reread every verb with its subject in Task 2.

Don’t

  • Don’t match the verb to the nearest noun.
  • Don’t write “informations” or “advices”.
  • Don’t say “people is”.
  • Don’t forget -s or -es with he, she, it.

Why we get it wrong

Mother-tongue transfer

Many languages do not change the verb for the subject, so checking is not a habit.

Try this: Say subject and verb together: the cost has, the costs have.

Distance

A long subject pushes the verb far away, and the nearest noun takes over.

Try this: Underline the head noun and draw an arrow to its verb.

Words that look plural

News and economics end in -s, so they feel plural.

Try this: Keep a short list of the singular ones and check it when you write.

Small habits that help

  • Underline every subject and verb in your last essay.
  • After writing, do one pass for -s endings, is/are and has/have only.
  • In Task 1, check every number, percentage and proportion phrase.

In IELTS and real life

  • Speaking: watch the he/she/it forms. My brother works, my parents work.
  • Writing: Task 1 phrases. The number of… has risen. A number of… have. The percentage of… is.
  • Reading: find the subject across long phrases to follow the meaning.
  • Listening: -s endings are weak. Listen for is/are and has/have instead.

Real world: emails and reports with correct agreement look careful and professional.

Agreement slips count against grammatical accuracy in Writing and Speaking. A few are normal, but repeated ones lower the score.

Practice

Spot the mistake

  1. The cost of living are rising.
    Show answerThe cost of living is rising.: cost is the subject.
  2. People is worried.
    Show answerPeople are worried.: people is plural.
  3. The news are bad.
    Show answerThe news is bad.: news is singular.
  4. A number of students is absent.
    Show answerA number of students are absent.: a number of means several.

Say it another way

  1. Use “the number of”: students / rising.
    Show answerThe number of students is rising.
  2. Use “a number of”: students / choosing online courses.
    Show answerA number of students are choosing online courses.

Fill the gaps

The advice (1) ___ (be) useful. Neither my parents nor my brother (2) ___ (know). There (3) ___ (be) several reasons. Each of the books (4) ___ (have) a cover. Most of the students (5) ___ (pass).

Show answers1 is · 2 knows · 3 are · 4 has · 5 pass

Speak and write

  • Say five sentences about your family using he/she/it and they. Check every verb ending.
  • Write six Task 1 sentences using the number of, a number of and the percentage of.

Making it stick

  • Pick one habit: after every paragraph, circle each subject and its verb and check they match.
  • Keep a small notebook of your own mistakes. Write the wrong sentence, the right one, and why. Read it every other day.
  • Redo the quiz after a day, three days and a week.
  • Read examples aloud, then write three about your own life.
