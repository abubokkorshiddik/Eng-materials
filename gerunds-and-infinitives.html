Intermediate level

Gerunds and infinitives

Some verbs are followed by the -ing form, others by to + verb. The first verb decides.

In plain words

After many verbs, the next verb needs a special form, and the verb before it decides which. Enjoy takes -ing (I enjoy reading). Want takes to (I want to read). You cannot work this out by logic. It is a matter of habit and chunks. But the lists are shorter than you fear, and a few groups cover most of the mistakes. Learn the groups, not single verbs.

How to build it

Gerund verb + -ing: enjoy swimming, avoid making

Infinitive to + base verb: want to go, decide to leave

Bare base verb alone: must go, let him go, make her laugh

After a preposition always -ing: interested in learning, good at cooking

When we use it

Verbs followed by -ing

I enjoy reading. She avoided answering.

enjoy, avoid, finish, suggest, consider, keep, mind, admit, practise, imagine, risk.

Verbs followed by to

I decided to apply. He refused to help.

want, need, hope, decide, plan, agree, refuse, manage, afford, promise, expect.

Prepositions followed by -ing

I am interested in learning Spanish.

interested in, good at, thinking of, instead of, before, after, by.

Purpose: to + verb

I went there to study.

Answers the question why.

Adjective + to

It is important to rest. I am happy to help.

Feelings and judgements are followed by to.

Same verb, different meaning

I stopped smoking.
I stopped to smoke.

Gave up the habit vs paused in order to smoke.

I remember meeting her.
Remember to lock the door.

A memory of the past vs a duty for later.

I tried calling her.
I tried to call her.

An experiment vs an attempt.

I like swimming.
I would like to swim.

General enjoyment vs a specific wish.

Like, love, hate, start, begin and continue can take either form with little change in meaning.

Mistakes people make

I enjoy to read. → I enjoy reading.

Enjoy is followed by -ing.

She suggested to go. → She suggested going.

Suggest is followed by -ing.

I want going. → I want to go.

Want is followed by to.

He is looking forward to meet you. → He is looking forward to meeting you.

Here to is a preposition, so use -ing.

I am used to get up early. → I am used to getting up early.

Be used to + -ing.

I decided going abroad. → I decided to go abroad.

Decide is followed by to.

She made me to laugh. → She made me laugh.

Make and let take the bare verb.

I want that you help me. → I want you to help me.

Want + person + to.

Going deeper

Core test: what comes before?

Look at the verb before. In the -ing list? Use -ing. In the to list? Use to. A preposition? Use -ing. A modal like can or must? Use the bare verb.

Verb + person + to

want him to go, ask her to help, tell them to wait, allow us to leave, advise you to rest. Not “want that he goes”.

Make, let, help

Make or let + person + bare verb: She made me wait. He let us go. Help allows both: help me (to) finish. In the passive, make takes to: I was made to wait.

Meaning changes

Stop, remember, forget, try and regret change meaning. I forgot to call (I never called). I forgot calling (I have no memory of calling).

To as a preposition

look forward to, be used to, object to, be committed to, in addition to, the key to: all followed by -ing. Test: can you put a noun there? I look forward to the holiday, so I look forward to meeting you.

Gerund as subject

Reading improves vocabulary. Learning a language takes time. The gerund works like a noun. An infinitive subject is more formal: To err is human.

Perfect and passive forms

He admitted having lied. She hopes to have finished by Friday. The children like being read to.

Shortcuts that work in exams

  • Feelings and stopping take -ing. enjoy, avoid, finish, mind, keep, practise.
  • Hopes and plans take to. want, hope, plan, decide, agree, refuse.
  • Prepositions take -ing. Always.
  • Look forward to + -ing. To is a preposition here.

Where exams try to catch you

  • “Used to” and “be used to”: used to + base verb (a past habit). be used to + -ing (familiar with).
  • “Suggest”: suggest + -ing, or suggest that he go. Never “suggest him to go”.
  • “Let”, “make”: let me go, make me go. But in the passive: I was made to go.
  • “Enjoy”: Needs -ing or an object: I enjoyed it. Not “I enjoyed” alone.

What to do, and what to leave alone

Do

  • Check the verb before.
  • Use -ing after prepositions.
  • Use to to show purpose.
  • Learn verbs in groups, not one by one.

Don’t

  • Don’t say “enjoy to”, “suggest to” or “avoid to”.
  • Don’t say “want that you…”.
  • Don’t use -ing after must or can.
  • Don’t forget that to is sometimes a preposition.

Why we get it wrong

Mother-tongue transfer

In many languages one form does both jobs, so there is no habit of choosing.

Try this: Learn pairs as chunks: enjoy + -ing.

So many exceptions

The lists are long and the logic is hidden.

Try this: Learn ten verbs per group first.

The word “to”

To looks like a sign of the infinitive, even when it is a preposition.

Try this: Test: if a noun fits after it, to is a preposition, so use -ing.

Small habits that help

  • Write five sentences with enjoy, avoid, finish, suggest and consider.
  • Make a two-column chart: verbs + -ing and verbs + to.
  • Record yourself and check every -ing and to form.

In IELTS and real life

  • Speaking: say “I enjoy reading” and “I’d like to travel” naturally.
  • Writing: use aim to, in order to, and by + -ing. “By investing in transport, cities can reduce traffic.”
  • Reading: spot the verb before the -ing or to form to follow the meaning.
  • Listening: to is a weak sound. Listen for it in plans and wishes.

Real world: plans, opinions and polite requests use these forms all the time.

This is one of the most common error types in IELTS Speaking and Writing, so it is worth drilling.

Practice

Spot the mistake

  1. I enjoy to read.
    Show answerI enjoy reading.: enjoy + -ing.
  2. She suggested to leave.
    Show answerShe suggested leaving.: suggest + -ing.
  3. I look forward to meet you.
    Show answerI look forward to meeting you.: to is a preposition.
  4. He made me to wait.
    Show answerHe made me wait.: make + bare verb.

Say it another way

  1. Fix it: “I want that you help me.”
    Show answerI want you to help me.
  2. Say it: “I paused in order to smoke.”
    Show answerI stopped to smoke.

Fill the gaps

I enjoy (1) ___ (read). She decided (2) ___ (leave). He is good at (3) ___ (cook). We went to the market (4) ___ (buy) fruit. I am looking forward to (5) ___ (see) you.

Show answers1 reading · 2 to leave · 3 cooking · 4 to buy · 5 seeing

Speak and write

  • Talk for one minute about hobbies using enjoy, like, love and hate + -ing.
  • Write six sentences about your plans using want, hope, plan and decide + to.

Making it stick

  • Make a two-column chart of verbs + -ing and verbs + to, and add three new verbs each week.
  • Keep a small notebook of your own mistakes. Write the wrong sentence, the right one, and why. Read it every other day.
  • Redo the quiz after a day, three days and a week.
  • Read examples aloud, then write three about your own life.
